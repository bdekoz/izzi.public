## Source data and curation

`scripts/hlt-local-stage2-dataset.py` extracts native 1080×1920 frames by
zero-based frame index at 24 fps from the eight **clean provider originals**.
It records source/image SHA-256 values, episode and clip positions, dimensions
and a simple dHash similarity screen. The screen is a review aid, not proof
of semantic uniqueness. No subtitles, wildlife overlays or manual rig
drawings enter this dataset.

The 37 candidates have these immutable source splits:

- Training candidates: clips 1–5 and 8.
- Validation: entire clip 6, including the region used by the accepted
  six-second performance reference.
- Test: entire clip 7.

Every crop inherits its source clip's split. This is one continuous route
with continuation-conditioned clips; the split does not establish unseen
locations or fully independent scenes. Boundary-adjacent frames from clips
5/8 are not in the curated training selection.

The reviewed pilot has **19 training crops from 9 source frames**, plus
two validation and two test group crops. Training comprises two group
views, two unicorn head views, six albatross views, five cat views and four
dog views. These are correlated examples, not 19 independent camera angles.
Context crops visibly retain neighboring animal fragments and captions
describe that context. The unicorn uses only the early yellow-horn views;
later darkened horns are excluded from its identity examples. Held-out
frames show source appearance and are not canonical horn-color targets.

Selection and captions:
`training-selection-v1.json`.
Native crops, manifests and training sidecars: `dataset/curated-v1/`.
Visual audit: `review/curated-contact.jpg` and eight `review/clip-*.jpg` sheets.
No transcript text was edited to produce image captions.

Tokens are ordinary prompt strings, with a frozen tokenizer/text encoder:
`hltstage4`, `hltunicorn`, `hltdog`, `hltalbatross`, `hltcat`.
The first adapter deliberately tests combined appearance learning. Its
uneven viewpoint/subject coverage is a limitation, not a production identity
lock. If it memorizes compositions or mixes markings, separate style and
individual-character adapters before expanding.

## Pilot and comparison recipe

The tracked configuration directory
uses only `curated-v1/train/`, rank/alpha 8, batch 1, 64 steps, learning rate
0.0001, four warmup steps, FP16 with residual scaling, cached text/latents,
CPU AdamW state, gradient/activation offload and two streamed blocks.
Target area is 512² with no upscaling of small native crops, no flips,
checkpointing at steps 32/64 and no automatic sample grid while training.
This is a bounded pilot, not a complete adapter search.
The data/provenance method can transfer to FLUX or Qwen. The adapter weights
are specific to MOSS and cannot be loaded into those image models or a
different video architecture.

Compare the unchanged base and final adapter with identical prompts,
seeds, image sizes and 50-step CFG-3 sampling. Include:

1. All four characters: count, species, horn, cyan collar, sun emblem and
   cyan/magenta bird markings.
2. Albatross/dog conversation: a new partner-facing pose; readable faces
   and closed listener mouth.
3. A single dog at a new angle: does the adapter summon unwanted animals
   or copy the canyon background despite the prompt?

Inspect images at useful viewing size. Record misses instead of promoting
falling training loss as visual success. A successful still-asset result
then feeds the existing six-second control timeline; a new audiovisual
preview must retain accepted gaze timing and exact dialogue before any
episode expansion. Do not generate each episode frame independently with
an image LoRA: neither a common seed nor an image adapter establishes
temporal consistency.

The matched comparison completed at 512×768 for the group/pair and 512²
for the single dog. Base generation took 329.761 seconds, versus 363.647
seconds with the adapter, excluding setup/teardown. Outer phase times were
364.701 and 403.798 seconds. Reported sampling peaks were 3.007/3.077 GiB
allocated. These six low-resolution images do not establish MOSS's quality
ceiling or a fair speed ranking against the differently configured Stage 1
Qwen/FLUX attempts.

Visual findings: both versions retain the requested animal counts. The
adapter removes the single dog's invented forehead marking and produces
stronger ink contours, while the group unicorn remains too stocky and the
perched bird retains an incorrect hooked/pouch-like beak. The paired dog
also drifts from white toward cream. These observations are recorded in
`review/appearance-assessment.json`; normal-speed video, exact gaze timing
and speaking-mouth motion were not tested in these stills. The next useful
appearance experiment is better character-specific viewpoint coverage and
training focus, followed by a small pose-library comparison. Preserve this
baseline rather than expanding it into an episode.
