## 3. Controlled performance design

Use layered 2D artwork and explicit transforms for the first proof. The
active pair should occupy a readable medium two-shot within the portrait
frame; keep the unicorn and cat visible as quiet supporting characters.
Judge the exchange both at native size and at **540×960 normal playback**.
Tiny eye shifts that require zooming do not pass.

### Assets and attachment

For the albatross and dog, prepare separate body, head, eye, mouth/beak and
foreground-occlusion layers, with documented pivots and consistent bounds.
The other two animals initially use quiet body/head layers. Start with
source cutouts and authored vector pose changes; use the image comparison
to decide whether generated alternatives improve the library.

Each active head needs forward and partner-facing drawings with intermediate
poses or a compatible mesh deformation. Rotating a flat raster only rotates
the drawing in its plane; it does not produce a new viewpoint. A simple
crossfade of whole heads can also produce doubled faces. Interpolate control
curves and use compatible pose geometry, redrawn transitions or a small
tracked mesh where necessary. Record any manual repair time.

The albatross gets a hinged beak with closed, small and larger apertures.
The dog keeps its mouth at rest. Eye direction and head pose are distinct
controls, both targeting the partner's head anchor rather than the camera.
Keep texture attached to the moving parts. Hierarchy:
`scene → body → neck/head → eyes and jaw/beak`, with explicit foreground masks.

New rig layers inherit their head transforms directly. If a saved generated
video is used as a moving plate, it needs independently measured tracking
and occlusion masks; the rig's transforms cannot be assumed to fit that video.
For Stage 1, prefer the newly composited cast over repairing every face in
the saved footage. Add a mild repeatable walk/flight cycle and restrained
background motion so the test also exposes attachment errors.

### Frame schedule

All intervals are zero-based, half-open, at 24 fps. The following is the
initial authored schedule, to be checked at normal playback:

| Frames | Time | Intended action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `[0, 6)` | 0–0.25 s | Establish the pair; mouths rest |
| `[6, 18)` | 0.25–0.75 s | Albatross eyes lead a clear head turn toward the dog |
| `[12, 24)` | 0.50–1.00 s | Dog returns the look, 0.25 seconds after the speaker's onset |
| `[24, 60)` | 1.00–2.50 s | Hold readable mutual attention for 1.5 seconds |
| `[60, 84)` | 2.50–3.50 s | Ease attention toward the path while speech continues |
| `[84, 120)` | 3.50–5.00 s | Settle; the beak continues to follow actual remaining speech |
| `[120, 144)` | 5.00–6.00 s | Quiet tail; all mouths rest |

Use smooth eased transitions without snapping. Start with roughly 20–35
degrees of *depicted* head orientation, adjusted to the actual drawings.
Screen-space rotations are not a substitute for that perspective change.
No wildlife event or unrelated listener reaction in the first proof.

Compute a smoothed speech-activity envelope from the existing WAV, using
short windows and separate open/close thresholds to avoid chatter. Tune
thresholds against this utterance, record them, and inspect internal pauses.
Map energy to a few beak apertures; do not use a constant sine wave. Use
the turn ownership and source sample boundaries as hard bounds, with actual
speech activity inside them. Exact visemes are a later refinement. The first
target is readable speech-timed articulation with onset/offset error no more
than **two frames, about 83 ms**, measured against audible speech.

## 7. Measurements and acceptance

Each receipt records the input/model/code hashes, host/device/runtime,
seed, dimensions, native fps/frame count, duration, precision, offload and
attention settings, status and failure reason. Measure model load, warm
inference, rasterization, transfer and encode separately using a monotonic
clock. Log wall time and both device allocator peaks and observed host RAM;
AMD shared-memory readings must identify their measurement source.
Record active manual cleanup time separately from unattended queue waits.

| Measure | Definition and use |
| --- | --- |
| Usable assets per worker-hour | Accepted pose assets divided by all active image-job hours, including failed attempts; show review/correction effort alongside it |
| Accepted candidate seconds per worker-hour | Sum of passing candidate durations divided by active rendering and repair hours; failed attempts stay in the denominator |
| Unique episode coverage | Report separately: repeated versions of this canary do not add new episode coverage; only 4.175 seconds of its audio comes from the source timeline |
| Speech ownership/timing | Only speaker 3's beak articulates; onset/offset error ≤2 frames against audible speech, with appropriate pause closures |
| Gaze | Intended pair and returned look visible at normal playback; a mutual hold of at least 36 frames, with no camera-facing substitute |
| Attachment and identity | No sliding/detached eyes or mouths, duplicate heads, species swaps, missing principals or lost defining markings |
| Technical delivery | Exactly 144 frames and 288,000 stereo audio frames; complete decoding, correct geometry and no missing/duplicated rendered frame ranges |

Computed head-target vectors and speech masks are diagnostic aids, not proof
that the picture reads correctly. Review the rendered result with sound at
normal size, then inspect slow playback and annotated frames for diagnosis.
Record expected cue times and observed cue times separately. Use `not_run`,
`runtime_failed`, `quality_failed`, `technical_pass`, and `human_approved`
as distinct states; missing measurements are null, never zero-speed successes.

Save a lossless FFV1/PCM master whose decoded audio matches the canary PCM
exactly, plus an H.264/AAC review proxy with codec delay accounted for and
checked alignment. Keep the exact caption line as an optional local layer;
do not replace its text with ASR. If captions obscure facial detail, the
review page can toggle an uncaptioned diagnostic view.

The final choice considers **performance control first**, then appearance
and total effort. R0 must show the required exchange. A model's attractive
texture cannot compensate for the wrong animal speaking or a missed gaze.
This small trial supports a production-method decision, not a general model
leaderboard or a reliable full-episode speed forecast.
