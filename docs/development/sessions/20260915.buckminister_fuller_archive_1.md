Its strongest idea is a repeated, compact record that places population, energy, material quantities, and their per-person counterparts in a consistent spatial relationship. Country and regional rows, year headings, subject headings, and a reusable key make this closer to a multivariate table with miniature diagrams than to a conventional plotted chart.

## Geometry and the meaning of an oblique matrix

### In simple terms

A matrix here means an arrangement of records in rows and columns, like a spreadsheet. In an ordinary spreadsheet the columns run straight down and meet the rows at right angles. In an **oblique matrix**, the columns run diagonally: each successive row is shifted sideways, so the cells are parallelograms. You still find a record by choosing a row and a column. Slanting the layout does not change its data.

Imagine sliding each horizontal strip of a spreadsheet a little farther sideways while keeping the strips horizontal. The result is a slanted table. Splitting each parallelogram diagonally can produce repeating triangles. Those triangles can then contain several labeled fields, much as a miniature form contains separate boxes.

In this photograph, horizontal country bands meet diagonal guides in both directions. Repeated triangular records occupy the denser left portion, while subject bands and sparse marks extend to the right. “Oblique matrix” is a useful geometric description of portions of this arrangement, not a verified historical name for the whole chart. The complete design is better described as a **triangular-lattice statistical atlas with oblique table regions and compound glyphs**. Its several guide families should not all be forced into one ordinary table interpretation.

The triangular lattice also does not establish that the diagram is a map, a chord diagram, a ternary plot, or a perspective drawing of three-dimensional data. In particular, three triangle corners do not by themselves mean three percentages summing to 100%.

### In mathematical terms

Let `c` be a column index and `r` a row index. Choose an origin **O** and two nonparallel step vectors **u** and **v**. A cell anchor is

```text
p(c, r) = O + c u + r v.

u = (w, 0)       one column step along a horizontal row
v = (s, h)       one row step, including its sideways shift

x = x0 + w c + s r
y = y0 + h r

[x - x0]   [w  s] [c]
[y - y0] = [0  h] [r]
```

For SVG, positive `y` points downward. Take `w > 0` and `h > 0`; `s` may have either sign. The rectangular case has `s = 0`. The oblique case has `s ≠ 0`, so `u · v = w s ≠ 0`: the basis vectors are not perpendicular. The determinant is `w h`, so nonzero `w` and `h` give an invertible layout. This is an affine mapping of table coordinates, not a new measurement scale.

For example, `O=(100,80)`, `u=(60,0)`, and `v=(30,52)` place column 2, row 3 at `(310,236)`. These numbers illustrate a renderer and are not measured from the photograph. The four corners of a cell are `p`, `p+u`, `p+u+v`, and `p+v`. Connecting `p+u` to `p+v` splits it into two triangles.

A regular equilateral triangular lattice is the special case

```text
u = (a, 0)
v = (a/2, sqrt(3) a/2).
```

Here `|u| = |v| = |v-u| = a`. The three undirected grid families follow `u`, `v`, and `v-u`, giving horizontal, +60°, and −60° lines. An alternative basis can follow the opposite diagonal. This supplies the photograph's general geometric vocabulary; its photographed angles cannot establish exact equilateral construction because of camera perspective, folds, and paper deformation.

Three line families on a plane are not three independent spatial dimensions. In lattice coordinates, rows have constant `r`, one diagonal family has constant `c`, and the other has constant `c+r`. The third is derived from the first two. A designer can assign a categorical role to a diagonal band, but that role needs a label and does not follow from geometry alone.

For hit testing, invert the map:

```text
r = (y - y0) / h
c = (x - x0 - s r) / w.
```

For an interior point, the floors identify its parallelogram cell. If `f_c` and `f_r` are its fractional coordinates, `f_c + f_r <= 1` selects the triangle adjacent to `p`; the other triangle is the complement. Define a consistent boundary rule for exact grid-line clicks.

An affine map preserves straightness, parallelism, and ratios along the same line. It generally changes angles and distances. Here all cell areas are `|w h|`, independent of `s`, but two obliquely oriented lengths cannot be compared as if they shared a Cartesian scale. Keep quantitative scales inside the record explicit.

A point inside an individual triangle with vertices `A`, `B`, and `C` can be located by barycentric coordinates:

```text
q = alpha A + beta B + gamma C
alpha + beta + gamma = 1; alpha, beta, gamma >= 0.
```

These weights are useful for placing labels and subdivisions. They become a *ternary data encoding* only if the plotted variables are three nonnegative components of a common total. Population, material mass, and energy do not meet that condition. The original key is most plausibly a set of fixed field positions, not such a composition plot.

### What the photograph's geometry accomplishes

The repeated downward-pointing triangles act as recognizable record containers. Dark compartments, fine internal rules, and small text give each record a consistent visual signature. Thick regional rules establish hierarchy; repeated headings along separated edges help track long diagonal bands. The large key enlarges the internal record layout and reserves a neighboring region for trend information.

There is no clear evidence that triangle area, the fraction of black ink, or the lengths of all visible strokes encode numerical magnitude. Some ink may distinguish fields or groups, and some strokes may be handwritten working notes. A faithful reconstruction must recover the legend's encoding rules before treating those features as measured quantities. Likewise, a blank area may be unfilled, unavailable, faded, or outside the intended scope; it is not automatically zero.

## Evaluation as a data visualization

The design is compelling as an expert reference atlas. It offers an overview of many places and topics while placing totals and per-person measures near one another. Its repeated local structure rewards learning: a reader who knows where a field lives can scan that position across records. Hierarchical bands and a consistent lattice could make a large physical wall chart navigable.

It is less effective for rapid numerical comparison. Tiny text carries much of the apparent information, and diagonal tracking requires effort. The compound key imposes a learning cost. Quantities with different units sit close together without a clearly recoverable common scale. Neither area nor length provides a demonstrably consistent quantitative channel in the photograph. A reader cannot safely conclude that one country has twice another's value from the apparent size or darkness of these records.

Two different problems coexist: the photograph makes an already dense design harder to read, and the design itself requires substantial decoding even with a perfect scan. Removing shadows addresses the first problem; larger type, simpler comparisons, and visible units address the second.

| Reader's task | Assessment | Suitable companion or alternative |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Inspect a place's broad resource profile | Strong potential once fields are learned | Enlarged record and exact-value table |
| Compare one measure across places | Weak at this density | Sorted bars or aligned dots with a common scale |
| Compare one measure over time | Possible, but requires tracking a field through many records | Aligned line-chart small multiples |
| Find broad multivariate patterns | Promising with a small stable set of fields | Matrix overview, filtering, shared per-metric scales |
| Read exact historical numbers | Poor in this photograph | Checked transcription linked to original image regions |
| Understand flows between places | No verified flow encoding here | A chord or flow view backed by actual origin–destination data |

For a new implementation, preserve the recognizable triangular record and regional hierarchy while making row/column highlighting, zoom, and record enlargement available. Keep text horizontal where possible. Use a conventional table for exact retrieval. Offer an orthogonal layout using the same data so users can judge whether the oblique presentation helps their task.

Use distinct treatments for observed, estimated, forecast, missing, not applicable, and unreadable values. Encode these with text or patterns as well as color. An accessible reading order should follow region, place, period, and metric rather than the order in which diagonal SVG paths were drawn. Provide a table as an alternative to navigating hundreds of tiny glyphs.

## Problems and datasets that could use this form

The best fit is a repeated multivariate record: a modest, stable group of related measures for each entity and period, with meaningful geographic or organizational grouping. The value lies in relating quantities within a record and comparing those profiles across the whole matrix. Obliqueness alone offers no analytical advantage.

| Problem | Record grain and fields | What the compound record helps reveal |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Resource planning | Region × year × scenario; population, electricity, material use, corresponding per-person quantities | Whether a change reflects population size, intensity of use, or both |
| Urban service provision | District × year; residents, water supplied, households served, service coverage, per-person use | High total provision alongside weak access or uneven distribution |
| Industrial resource efficiency | Plant × month; output, energy, water, scrap, per-unit intensities | Output growth accompanied by rising or falling resource intensity |
| Infrastructure planning | Service area × scenario; demand, capacity, utilization, shortfall | Capacity constraints and tradeoffs between scenarios |
| Ecological monitoring | Catchment × season; area, runoff, pollutant load, concentration, sampling coverage | Large loads versus high concentrations, with explicit measurement completeness |
| Archival research | Place × historical period; raw transcription, interpreted field, evidence region, review status | How much of an atlas is recovered and where interpretations remain unresolved |

These are proposed applications, not findings from the pictured values. For access and efficiency measures, retain their denominators. Compute a regional per-capita value as the sum of compatible totals divided by the sum of their populations, not the unweighted mean of country ratios. Do not combine disjoint periods, incompatible boundaries, or different energy definitions into one total.

Avoid this form when the main task is precise ranking, when there are very few values, when fields change constantly, or when an enormous time sequence would compress type beyond legibility. For a true three-part composition use a ternary plot; for actual trade/transfer relationships use a flow or chord chart. Neither relationship can be inferred from this grid's triangular appearance.

### Architecture

Add a sibling `src/izzi-svg-graphs-matrix.h` for the new rendering family. Put general lattice math in `izzi-svg-grid-matrix-systems.h` or a small dependency-free geometry header. Follow the typed graph model for the dataset and layout specification. The public name should describe the capability, for example `oblique_matrix`, with `world_chart` as a preset.

Separate three stages:

```text
reviewed records + metric definitions
    -> validated matrix model and resolved geometry
    -> SVG polygons, rules, labels, optional local plots, and a table
```

All numerical meaning must come from the records and metric definitions. The photograph can supply layout inspiration or a manually checked transcription; it must not supply values by measuring unverified triangle areas.

Suggested model fields:

| Type | Required information |
| --- | --- |
| Entity | Stable ID, label, parent region/group, historical boundary/version when applicable |
| Column | Stable ID, label, kind (`period` or `subject`), optional period and scenario; avoid making subject columns look like elapsed time |
| Metric | Stable ID, label, unit, denominator definition, scale/domain, formatting and valid range |
| Value | Optional numeric value, raw transcription, separate observation status and transcription-review status, source reference and optional original-pixel rectangle |
| Cell | Entity ID, column ID, metric/value records; independent source status for an entirely unfilled cell |
| Glyph template | Fixed polygons/label anchors and metric-to-slot bindings; no implicit interpretation of black area |
| Layout | Origin, basis vectors, row and column order, group gaps, cell size, typography, grid styles, label policy, viewport and margins |

Use independent observation and transcription states: a faithfully transcribed number may still be a historical forecast. A missing field must remain distinct from `0`. For a modern dataset, numerical values can be verified without ever having a raw archival transcription.

Illustrative API sketch only; these types/functions do not currently exist:

```cpp
namespace svg::graph {

    extent viewport{2400.0, 1600.0};
    point origin{260.0, 180.0};
    point column_step{96.0, 0.0};
    point row_step{48.0, 83.1384387633};
    double minimum_label_size{14.0};
    bool show_group_bands{true};
  };

  // Proposed interfaces, with matrix-specific records and scene primitives.
  matrix_layout_result
  layout_oblique_matrix(const matrix_dataset&, const oblique_matrix_spec&);
}

// Proposed SVG adapter; SVG primitives remain outside the data model.
svg::svg_element
emit_oblique_matrix(const svg::graph::matrix_scene&, const std::string& stem);
```

The anchor calculation can be a pure function using the equation above. Compute the viewport bounds from all transformed corners, labels, legend, and group gaps; a sheared table can extend outside the rectangular matrix's original bounds. Reject nearly collinear basis vectors, not just exactly zero determinants, to avoid unstable inverse mapping.

Construct glyph subdivisions from a canonical triangle using barycentric anchors. Draw polygons and rules first, then place text at transformed positions without shearing the glyphs of the text itself. Shearing an entire group containing labels would make text harder to read. Permit deliberate rotated headers while keeping numerical values upright. Generate the legend from the same slot definitions as the records so it cannot silently disagree with them.

### Changes associated with the three requested headers

1. **Line:** factor or add a small explicit-rectangle scale/projector and a plot-only line emitter. Allow a matrix cell to contain a sparkline without inheriting the current 200-pixel graph margins or axes. Preserve the old API through defaults or overloads. Split lines at missing values, and share a metric's domain across comparable cells. Keep time spacing truthful.

2. **Bar:** add a reusable local bar-mark helper, or place it in a shared marks header, taking a box, baseline, value, and explicit domain. This supports tiny total/per-capita bars in a redesigned record. Do not repurpose `histogram_bin` for unrelated fields. Absolute bars need a zero baseline; different units need separate labeled scales.

3. **Chord:** no modification is necessary for a faithful matrix renderer. If new data includes transfers between places, expose a linked view using the existing typed `flow_dataset` and `linear_chord_spec`, with stable shared entity IDs. A connector overlay would require actual relations, an explicit weight scale, and a policy for crossings. Do not turn triangle borders into chords or introduce an alpha60 dependency.

The new matrix scene needs polygons, text anchors, metric bindings, and cell identifiers; existing graph nodes with only a position/radius cannot express the whole structure. If integrating into `graph_document`, update the dataset/layout variants, validation dispatch, `scene_kind`, JSON serialization in `izzi-svg-graph-json.h`, and corresponding schemas/fixtures together. Choose and document schema compatibility explicitly. For an initial prototype, a separate matrix document avoids destabilizing the existing `izzi.graph/1` contract.

### Encoding and interaction rules

Offer two explicit modes. An archival mode reproduces verified field positions, labels, and transcribed values, including unresolved regions. An analytical mode uses the same lattice but reduces each glyph to a few well-labeled values or local plots. Document which visual choices belong to the new design.

If triangle area is deliberately introduced as a quantitative encoding in the new mode, scaling a similar triangle's linear dimensions by `k` changes area by `k²`; proportional area therefore requires `k = sqrt(value / maximum)` for nonnegative values. Filling a triangular container to a height also gives a nonlinear area fraction. Use this only with a clearly stated rule; an aligned bar is usually easier to compare. Do not attribute either encoding to the original without evidence.

Use stable SVG groups and IDs for each cell/field, `<title>`/`<desc>` for the image, and a companion accessible table. A browser wrapper can add keyboard focus, row/column highlighting, and a visible detail panel with value, unit, period, status, and source region. Exported SVG must remain intelligible without scripts, hover, or color.

### Implementation order and verification

First implement the lattice transform, canonical cell, generated legend, and a small fixture explicitly labeled as geometry test data. Then render a few manually verified archival labels and unresolved-value placeholders. Add local line/bar marks only when their metric definitions are available. Wire the typed/JSON facade after the geometry and model are stable. A full transcription of this sheet is a separate data-recovery task.

Meaningful checks include forward/inverse coordinate round trips, both slant directions, triangular boundary ownership, near-singular rejection, finite coordinates, viewport containment, duplicate IDs, unresolved references, missing-versus-zero behavior, and escaped SVG text. Include all-empty and constant-value local plots and prevent lines from bridging missing observations. Check multi-translation-unit inclusion if adding header-defined functions, following the existing graph ODR tests.

Inspect one exported page at its intended reading size, a zoomed record, and a grayscale rendering for clipped labels, broken group alignment, illegible numerals, and a legend that matches the cells. Test the existing line, histogram, and graph-model paths after changing shared helpers. These are proposed implementation checks; no new renderer was built or tested during this analysis.

The useful extension is a reusable multivariate matrix with explicit geometry and data semantics. It can reproduce the chart's arrangement while giving modern readers legible numbers, inspectable provenance, and conventional views for precise comparisons.

## Application to Alpha60 Olympics GeoJSON: three approaches

Added 2026-09-15. These proposals map the actual `feature.properties.downloaders` fields to the chart's repeated-record idea. They are design suggestions, not an implemented visualization. Sources inspected: the [Olympics page](https://alpha60-devops.github.io/alpha60-results-sports/docs/olympics.html), its [v7.4 map renderer](https://alpha60-devops.github.io/alpha60-results-sports/resources/izzi-map-leaflet-geojson-v7.4.js), and its linked [Week 1 GeoJSON](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/alpha60-devops/alpha60-results-sports/main/data/olympics-2026-week-00001.geojson.gz). ### Actual fields and their interpretation

The menu offers `size`, `mobile`, `satellite`, `tor`, `tor_exit_nodes`, `vpn`, `relay`, `proxy`, `hosting`, and `service`, under either `downloaders` or `uploaders`. The proposed default is downloaders. Here `size` is the total downloader count reported for the feature, **not file size in bytes**. The remaining fields are network-characteristic counts; their precise classification rules require the producer's definitions. In particular, do not assume `service` is the sum of VPN, Tor, proxy, and relay. These access paths and labels are explicit in the [map renderer](https://alpha60-devops.github.io/alpha60-results-sports/resources/izzi-map-leaflet-geojson-v7.4.js).

For feature `f`, time window `t`, and a network field `k`, define:

```text
N(f,t)   = feature.properties.downloaders.size
Ck(f,t)  = feature.properties.downloaders[k]
Pk(f,t)  = 100 × Ck(f,t) / N(f,t), when N > 0 and both fields are valid.
```

`Pk` is a percentage of the reported downloader count for that feature/window. It is not population penetration, share of all Internet users, a share of bytes, or proof of distinct people. Preserve the producer's counting semantics; aggregation cannot establish uniqueness that the input does not establish.

| “Visualize By” field | Suggested representation inside the new design |
| --- | --- |
| `size` | Prominent exact count plus a separately labeled total-count scale |
| `mobile`, `satellite` | Separate count or percentage slots for access characteristics |
| `tor`, `tor_exit_nodes` | Separate slots; do not add them or assume a subset relationship without a definition |
| `vpn`, `relay`, `proxy` | Independent count or percentage slots |
| `hosting`, `service` | Independent slots, retaining the original field names until definitions are documented |

The focused data-quality check matters to the geometry. In Week 1, 102 top-level features have a sum of network counts greater than `size`, although no individual network count exceeds its feature's size. Treat the fields as potentially overlapping memberships, not an exhaustive partition. Equal *layout* compartments are fine; a pie, 100% stack, or ternary position implying mutually exclusive components is not supported. [Week 1 source](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/alpha60-devops/alpha60-results-sports/main/data/olympics-2026-week-00001.geojson.gz).

### Approach 1 — Place-by-week atlas with compound triangular records

This is the closest adaptation of the Fuller sheet. Make rows geographic units, grouped by country, and columns weekly windows. Each row–week intersection contains one fixed-size triangular record. A selector can switch the row unit between H3 cells and explicitly aggregated cities/countries; it must not silently mix them.

Use a prominent upper compartment for `N`, then two independently labeled compartments for selected network fields, initially VPN and mobile. Display both count and percentage when zoomed, for example `611 / 27,177 · 2.25%`. Use separate fixed-length microbars for the two percentages. Additional fields appear in an enlarged record or adjacent detail panel, with all ten fields available in a stable order. A triangle's silhouette and compartment sizes stay constant; the compartments are containers, not proportional pieces of the total.

The original chart's “total versus per-capita” relationship becomes “total observed downloaders versus the fraction with a selected network characteristic.” This borrows its comparative logic without claiming that downloader shares are demographic per-capita measures.

Read horizontally to ask whether a place's downloader volume or VPN fraction changed across weeks. Read vertically to compare places within the same window. Keep the row order fixed across weeks; use one percentage scale of 0–100% and a separate shared total-count scale. Show partial windows explicitly. If a feature is absent from a week, show “not present in source” until its absence can be interpreted; do not join it to the preceding point as zero.

**Best for:** changes over time and places whose totals and network profiles move differently. **Tradeoff:** two or three visible fields per small record are practical; ten are too dense. Start with selected places or country groups, with expansion into individual H3 rows.

**Izzi path:** use the proposed oblique matrix geometry and group bands. Draw local microbars with the bar helper and, optionally, a selected row's trend with the line helper. Feed the record and its enlarged legend from the same metric-slot definitions.

### Approach 2 — Place-by-metric matrix of paired triangles

This is the recommended first implementation: it works with a single weekly file and makes every “Visualize By” field comparable at once. Rows are selected geographic units; columns are the ten fields in the menu order. Pin `size` as the first column. Make each remaining cell a rhombus split into two triangles: one holds the absolute network count and the other its percentage of that row's downloader total.

Give the two triangles consistent meanings throughout the matrix, with a clear enlarged key. Use a separate legend for count intensity or count bars, and a common 0–100% scale for percentage intensity or bars. Label any logarithmic count scale. The `size` column needs only the total; its second triangle can be unused rather than displaying an uninformative 100%. Keep text horizontal and show exact values on focus and in a table.

This directly answers two different questions: “Where is the largest VPN-associated count?” and “Where does VPN account for the largest fraction of the reported downloaders?” A high count and a high percentage need not identify the same place. The triangular split keeps those two readings together, but a rectangular two-line cell should remain available if it is easier to read.

For an actual example, the Seoul-labeled Week 1 feature has `size=27,177` and `vpn=611`, giving 2.25%. One Amsterdam-labeled feature has `size=10,762` and `vpn=3,867`, giving 35.93%. That Amsterdam feature also has `hosting=9,228` and `service=2,218`; these are separate comparisons against 10,762, not slices to add together. The file contains another Amsterdam-labeled feature, so this example is **not an Amsterdam city total**. [Inspected feature records](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/alpha60-devops/alpha60-results-sports/main/data/olympics-2026-week-00001.geojson.gz).

**Best for:** discovering profiles across all ten fields and comparing absolute volume with relative prevalence. **Tradeoff:** counts can be highly skewed and percentages can be unstable for tiny denominators. Keep `N` visible and offer an explicit minimum-count filter with its threshold displayed.

**Izzi path:** this primarily requires matrix polygons, text, and scales. The bar helper is optional; no chord renderer is needed. The same reviewed records can drive a sorted bar view for a selected column and a line view after multiple windows are supplied.

### Approach 3 — Geographic map linked to an enlarged triangular record

Retain the existing geographic map to answer “where,” and use a Fuller-style triangular record as the selected feature's detail view. Select a map point to open a large glyph showing its total and independently labeled network fields. Group fields visually into access characteristics and other network classifications, while making clear that these are navigation groups rather than additive totals.

At low zoom, the map can retain one selected metric, such as VPN count. At high zoom, small fixed-size triangular summary markers could show total plus one or two selected shares; move the full ten-field record into the detail panel so text remains readable. A companion oblique strip can show the selected feature over time. Preserve true projected geographic positions, and link all views through the stable feature key.

The current renderer merges points according to selected metric, marker overlap, zoom, and distance. Such display clusters are unsuitable as persistent matrix rows: their membership can change when the user selects VPN instead of size. Use original features or a fixed, documented geographic aggregation for the atlas. If a map cluster is selected, label it as a temporary group and compute its detail using the same original member features for every field. [Current filtering and reduction logic](https://alpha60-devops.github.io/alpha60-results-sports/resources/izzi-map-leaflet-geojson-v7.4.js).

**Best for:** geographic exploration followed by multivariate inspection. **Tradeoff:** the map cannot support dense, simultaneous ten-field comparisons; the matrix or detail panel carries that work.

**Izzi path:** generate the record as a reusable SVG group or standalone SVG, with geometry anchors and labels independent of map projection. The map wrapper owns selection and geographic projection; the SVG renderer owns the compound record. No geographic transfer links should be invented from these aggregate counts, so chord rendering still requires a separate origin–destination dataset.

### Shared adapter and data rules

The Week 1 source declares `swarm_geo_partition_by="hexagon"`, resolution 5, and `datestamp="2026-02-08-to-2026-02-14-partial"`. Its 7,884 top-level features have unique H3 values in the inspected file, but 41 country/geoname combinations occur more than once. Geographic labels therefore cannot serve as unique feature keys. It also contains 647 nested `collection_week_by_btiha` collections: select either the top-level collection or a specified nested media collection; recursively collecting both would mix scopes. [Dataset and metadata](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/alpha60-devops/alpha60-results-sports/main/data/olympics-2026-week-00001.geojson.gz).

The adapter should produce this conceptual record, retaining one row per scope, geographic unit, window, and category:

```text
scope_id, dataset_id, duration_index, datestamp, partial_window
geography_kind, geography_id, country_code, city, coordinates
category = downloaders | uploaders
size, mobile, satellite, tor, tor_exit_nodes, vpn, relay, proxy, hosting, service
field_status, source_url, data_version
```

Use lossless H3 handling: the source encodes H3 IDs as JSON integers larger than JavaScript's safe integer range. Parse them losslessly, or convert them to strings using a lossless parser before sending them to JavaScript. Calling `String()` after ordinary `JSON.parse()` cannot restore lost low bits. A C++ adapter can retain an unsigned 64-bit value and serialize its identifier as a string. Include scope and period in record IDs; preserve H3 as the geographic join key within a consistent resolution.

For a selected group `G`, compute each percentage as `100 × sum(Ck) / sum(N)` over the same member records. Do not average feature percentages. Do not add privacy/network fields into a combined “protected users” count, compute `size - sum(network fields)` as “fiber,” or infer intersections such as VPN-and-hosting from marginal counts. Those operations require additional classification or joint-count data. Totals across weeks likewise need an explicit counting definition and must not be labeled unique people.

Validate missingness before division. Unlike the existing map helper, which returns zero for missing/null properties and hides values below one, the matrix should distinguish explicit zero, missing, invalid, and absent feature. The source's `swarm_size_min=3` signals a threshold whose exact filtering rule needs confirmation; an absent feature should not imply no downloaders. Respect actual metadata instead of relying only on link captions: some page captions and their URL week numbers differ.

The scoped quality check found all ten numeric fields present in every top-level downloader record, no negative or fractional counts, and no zero `size` denominators in this sample. Those observations justify the illustrative ratios, not assumptions about other weeks or nested collections. The `data_version` inspected was `20260628`; the URLs track mutable repository content. No historical change, producer-level deduplication, or classification accuracy was validated.

Reproduce the principal checks with the inspected source; this query deliberately excludes nested media collections:

```sh
curl -fsSL 'https://raw.githubusercontent.com/alpha60-devops/alpha60-results-sports/main/data/olympics-2026-week-00001.geojson.gz' |
  gzip -dc |
  jq '{
    features: (.features | length),
    repeated_city_keys: ([.features |
      group_by([.properties.country_code, .properties.geoname_id])[] |
      select(length > 1)] | length),
    network_sums_above_size: ([.features[].properties.downloaders |
      select(([del(.size)[]] | add) > .size)] | length)
  }'
# Inspected output: 7884 features, 41 repeated city keys,
# 102 records with network sums above size.
```

Start with **Approach 2** for one week and a manageable set of features or explicitly aggregated countries. It exposes all fields and verifies the count/share semantics with little interaction machinery. Add the weekly arrangement in Approach 1 when cross-window identity and coverage are validated; use Approach 3 when geographic context is the main entry point. The data-quality skill informed the non-additive encodings, stable-key recommendation, and explicit scope/missingness rules.

## Approach 2 implementation and trial

Implemented 2026-09-15 following the unattended implementation request. The earlier sections describe proposals; this section records the delivered subset. The [API and usage guide](20260915.count-share-matrix.md) includes build instructions, CLI options, data contracts, and verification commands.

The trial renders the twelve largest top-level downloader H3 features in the supplied Olympics Week 1 file, with all ten metric columns. Each fixed-size rhombus contains an upper-left count triangle and a lower-right percentage triangle. Counts use a shared logarithmic color scale; percentages use a fixed 0–100% scale. Values remain independently labeled, so overlapping network classifications are not presented as an additive composition.
