## Projector facts and feasibility

The [manufacturer's manual, pp. 3 and 12–14](https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/0568/7024/0322/files/RODPJS450_Manual_EN.pdf?v=1740554591)
specifies 1920 × 1080 resolution and 200 ANSI lumens, and describes digital
zoom, keystone controls, projector orientation, USB playback, and HDMI input.
The [manufacturer's LUMA 450 kit specifications](https://kodakprojectors.caglobal.com/products/kodak-luma-450-basic-projector-kit)
give a 1.2:1 throw ratio, 16:9 aspect ratio, and 100% offset. Treat the optical
values as nominal starting points and verify them on the actual projector.

The [product listing](https://www.kodakphotoplus.com/collections/projectors/products/kodak-luma-450-portable-full-hd-smart-projector)
advertises a maximum 150-inch image. **Neither requested square fits inside
that advertised size**: the delivered 3 m and 2 m layouts require full-frame
diagonals of 246.4 and 164.3 inches respectively. The 2 m version substantially
reduces the required room depth, but remains outside the advertised range.
Focus, usable brightness and edge sharpness at these sizes require an actual
wall trial; the files and calculations do not establish that the projector
can deliver them acceptably.

A [separate Kodak promotional page](https://www.kodakphotoplus.com/pages/kodak-luma-450-portable-full-hd-smart-projector)
claims 170 inches. The 2 m layout fits that claim. This plan uses the lower
150-inch product-listing value conservatively; the conflicting listings
make a trial with the actual unit necessary rather than establishing a
precise optical cutoff.

At a 150-inch 16:9 diagonal, the full image height is about 1.868 m and the
square in these files would be only **1.826 m** across. A 1.8 m square would
fit within that size, but shrinking either delivered video also shrinks its
tick spacing and changes the physical blue-line height. It would require a
newly calibrated layout to retain truthful metric marks.

## Geometry and the distance X

Both assets use a 1920 × 1080 frame with square pixels. The square measures
1056 × 1056 pixels, from `(432, 12)` to `(1488, 1068)`. The 12-pixel vertical
margins preserve the full outer stroke. The black sidebars are part of the
projected image; its entire width determines throw distance.

For square side `S` metres, with zoom at its original full image size:

```text
pixels per metre p = 1056 / S
full image width W = 1920 / p
full image height H = 1080 / p
lens-to-wall distance X = 1.2 × W
vertical black margin b = 12 / p
blue-line raster y = 1068 − (1.524 − grid_bottom_height) × p
```

| Quantity | 3 × 3 m | 2 × 2 m |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Lens-to-wall distance X | **6.545 m / 21 ft 5.7 in** | **4.364 m / 14 ft 3.8 in** |
| Full image width | 5.455 m | 3.636 m |
| Full image height | 3.068 m | 2.045 m |
| Full image diagonal | 246.4 in | 164.3 in |
| Black sidebar width, each | 1.227 m | 0.818 m |
| Black top/bottom margin, each | 34.09 mm | 22.73 mm |
| Green line spacing in file | 352 px | 528 px |
| Red tick spacing in file | 176 px | 264 px |
| Blue-line y, measured from frame top | 531.552 px | 263.328 px |
| Wall distance represented by one pixel | 2.841 mm | 1.894 mm |
| Grid wall area | 9 m² | 4 m² |

Reducing the square from 3 m to 2 m multiplies X by **2/3**, saving 2.182 m
of throw. These are optical estimates measured from the lens, not the back
of the case. With no border margin the theoretical distances would be
6.400 m and 4.267 m; use the values above for the delivered files.

## Room placement and projector height

Room dimensions were not supplied. The following are setup requirements,
not a confirmation that a particular room fits.

For the default floor-origin grid, use an **inverted, level front projection
mount**. Interpreting the stated 100% offset as the lens axis meeting the
image edge, the inverted lens aligns with the top of the full image:

```text
inverted lens height = grid bottom + S + b
upright lens height = grid bottom − b
```

| Placement item | 3 × 3 m | 2 × 2 m |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Suggested inverted lens-centre height | **3.034 m / 9 ft 11.5 in above floor** | **2.023 m / 6 ft 7.6 in above floor** |
| Lens position from the square's left edge | 1.500 m / 4 ft 11.1 in | 1.000 m / 3 ft 3.4 in |
| Grid top above floor | 3.000 m / 9 ft 10.1 in | 2.000 m / 6 ft 6.7 in |
| Working room-depth allowance | About 6.9 m / 22 ft 7.7 in | About 4.7 m / 15 ft 5 in |

The room-depth allowance adds roughly 35 cm behind the lens for the case,
cables and placement adjustment; measure the actual installation. The
ceiling must clear the lens height plus the actual body/mount and ventilation
space. To keep the entire blue line on one flat wall, allow the full image
width listed above: 5.455 m for the 3 m grid, or 3.636 m for the 2 m grid.
The lower black margin extends below the floor-level grid;
the calibrated green border itself remains at the floor/wall boundary.

Keep the projector level and its optical axis perpendicular to the wall.
Use front-ceiling orientation for the inverted installation. Verify where
the lens axis meets the actual image before fixing the mount: the published
offset percentage is a starting assumption, not a measured mounting datum.

An upright projector cannot put this default square at floor level while
remaining level: its calculated lens height would be below the floor. For
an easier low-stand installation, regenerate with the grid bottom **15 cm
above the floor**. This gives upright lens heights of about **11.6 cm for
3 m**, or **12.7 cm for 2 m**. The grid tops become 3.15 m and 2.15 m;
the blue line stays at 1.524 m above the floor because the generator moves
it within the grid. Heights refer to the lens centre, not the stand top.
When the raised grid permits a non-negative upright lens height, its
positioning labels automatically show that upright setup in both units.

## Calibration and playback plan

1. Begin with the 2 m plate. Measure the available wall height, width and
   lens-to-wall depth against the table. Mark the intended square corners,
   50 cm intervals and the 1.524 m height on the wall using a tape measure.
2. Darken the room. Use external power and set brightness for a visible
   calibration image. Focus at the intended distance before committing to
   the installation, since both requested sizes exceed the advertised range.
3. Set output to 1920 × 1080, square pixels, original 16:9 framing, with
   overscan/cropping disabled. Set projector zoom to the original full-size
   image and keystone corrections to zero/off. Align the hardware physically.
4. Place the lens at the calculated X and height. Measure the outer green
   border **centreline to centreline** horizontally and vertically. Adjust
   throw using `new_X = current_X × target_side / measured_side`. Re-focus
   after moving it. Unequal width and height indicate an alignment or aspect
   problem; correct that before adjusting scale again.
5. Check 1 m green intervals, 50 cm red intervals, parallel sides, and equal
   corner-to-corner diagonals: 4.2426 m for the 3 m square or 2.8284 m for
   the 2 m square. Aim for agreement within about 1 cm over the whole grid;
   the pixel scale and projected stroke thickness limit finer measurements.
6. Measure the centre of the blue line at 1.524 m above the floor at both
   ends. Correct mounting height/level and recheck the square. Confirm the
   same geometry when switching from the SVG/PNG to the MKV.
7. Play the MKV full-screen and enable repeat-one in the player. Hide player
   controls and the pointer. The file contains exactly 900 unchanged frames
   at 30 fps, with no fade or black transition. Repeat is a player setting;
   verify the player itself does not flash an overlay or blank the output at
   the 30-second restart. A connected HDMI player is the fallback if the
   projector's built-in USB player does not accept this MKV or loop cleanly.

The video uses H.264 High, 8-bit `yuv420p`, BT.709 and limited-range video
levels. Matching video levels on playback preserve black. Thin saturated
strokes soften slightly through 4:2:0 sampling and projection; use the
SVG/PNG as the uncompressed geometry reference. `1 pt` and `2 pt` are SVG
typographic widths at 96 pixels/inch: 1.333 and 2.667 pixels. At the 3 m
scale these project to approximately 3.79 and 7.58 mm; at 2 m, 2.53 and
5.05 mm. They do not represent literal 0.353/0.706 mm strokes on the wall.
