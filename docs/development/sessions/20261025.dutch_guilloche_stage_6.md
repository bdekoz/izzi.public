#### 4.2 Elliptical loop field

First test the existing elliptical Lissajous carrier with family modulation
on a small reference patch. Its radial-only modulation may be insufficient
to reproduce the alternating lenses, pinches, and loop crossings. The proposed
extension is an `elliptic_loop_spec` with independently controlled normal and
tangential oscillation around a closed elliptical carrier:

```text
C_i(t) = (cx + a_i cos(t), cy + b_i sin(t))
P_i(t) = C_i(t)
         + A_n(i) q_n(k_i t + phi_i) N_i(t)
         + A_t(i) q_t(k_i t + phi_i) T_i(t),  0 <= t <= 2*pi
```

Here `T_i` and `N_i` are unit tangent and normal vectors; `q_n` and `q_t`
are bounded periodic harmonic functions. Integer cycle counts give closed
paths. Ring spacing, ellipse aspect, amplitude, phase progression, and a
small set of harmonics determine the lenses and diamonds. Resolve points in
final drawing coordinates so nonuniform ellipse scaling does not accidentally
change stroke weight.

Compare radial-only and normal/tangential models against the measured patches
before choosing one. Retain the simplest model that matches both the outer
and inner field. Cover the whole panel and clip the excess at its boundary.
Model changes in density through path placement; match ink width separately.
Starting searches may use 48–128 field contours and 32–128 cycles, adjusted
to measured spacing rather than treated as final historical counts.

#### 4.3 Cartouche, connectors, and rosettes

- Build the central oval as separate boundary, woven surround, and hatched
  interior components. Fit the hatch direction and pitch, the clear boundary
  gaps, and the outline of `1000`. Use contour-aware hatching or clipping for
  the numeral's light and dark structure. Match the silhouette first, then
  its interior engraving.
- Fit a smooth width envelope to each connector. Try existing harmonic-wave
  families first; if their scalar envelope cannot express the rounded opening
  and pinched transitions, add an `envelope_band_spec` with a bounded smooth
  profile, strand spacing, phase progression, and loop amplitudes. Shared
  endpoint positions and tangents should make component joins continuous.
- Fit the rosettes as a small floral core plus one or more rationally closed
  roulette families and a dense rim. Compare candidate frequency ratios in
  isolated close-ups. The observed layered web is the fitting target; a
  single generic rose does not supply all of those layers.
- Make left/right symmetry the initial model. Add only low-amplitude,
  explicitly recorded asymmetry where the corrected image supports it.
- Determine overlap and knockout boundaries from the photograph. Reuse
  polygon clips for positive apertures; where a compound exclusion is needed,
  implement an explicit mask or path clipping operation. Painting paper over
  an overlap must not accidentally erase later hatching or lettering.
