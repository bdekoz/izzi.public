
Investigate hardware and software options for installing 3 displays
stacked on top of each other in portrait orientation. This is called a "3 x Vertical display stack."

Details:
- The three displays are all Dell U2718Q models.
- The port to use is displayport or micro-displayport
- available hardware is a rasberry pi 5, and a low-profile NUC form factor.
- each monitor has a VESA mount in the back.

The video loops are all 4k vertical, similar to the work done by this project and outputs from 

20261004.decasia_agesia_version_4_filmspace.md
20260929.moire_glitch_motion.md

Evaluate options for running the three screens with frame-accurate playback, so that the three videos can be accurately run "as if" one big screen, where effects from screen one can move from screen 1, through screen 2, and into screen 3.

How are the videos synchronized when running to prevent drift? What's the most accurate frame synchronization that is possible? What is the cheapest hardware hoption?

As a historical reference, consider a "2 x horizontal display stack" where two 1080p Samsung 27" smart tvs are installed landscape, one on top of each other. These are no synchronized, but instead play loops from a mounted USB drive that contains video loops, and the smart TV's sofware is used to read the USB media and play the video, and loop. This is not frame accurate, but is inexpensive.


Append software/hardware setups, suggest 1-5 options.

## Researched proposal — October 20, 2026

**Recommendation: first qualify the existing NUC as a single player for all
three displays.** This is the cheapest plausible route to coordinated
playback, provided its actual GPU and ports support the required outputs.
Use a single playback timeline and test the visible seams. If the work
requires hardware-locked output timing at native 4K, move to a compatible
workstation with a synchronization board. A BrightSign XC4055 is the more
compact appliance alternative, subject to testing its HDMI-to-DisplayPort
converters. A Datapath Fx4 is useful when lower resolution is acceptable.

These are researched designs, not measurements of the available hardware.
The NUC model, operating system, GPU, connectors, and decoder capabilities
are unspecified. No purchase, installation, or hardware configuration was
performed as part of this proposal.

### Geometry, existing media, and connections

Interpret “3 x Vertical display stack” literally: three portrait monitors,
one above another. Number them **1 = top, 2 = middle, 3 = bottom**.

| Item | Native 4K installation | Lower-resolution pilot |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Picture per portrait panel | 2160 × 3840 | 1080 × 1920, scaled to the panel |
| Logical wall, excluding bezels | 2160 × 11520 | 1080 × 5760 |
| Total picture pixels per frame | 24,883,200 | 6,220,800 |
| Initial source cadence | 30 fps | 30 fps |
| Preferred output refresh | Identical 60.000 Hz on all outputs | Identical 60.000 Hz on all outputs |

The two referenced production notes actually document **1080 × 1920,
30 fps** deliveries: [filmspace version 4](20261004.decasia_agesia_version_4_filmspace.md)
is a silent 5,400-frame / 180-second HEVC movie, and
[moiré glitch motion](20260929.moire_glitch_motion.md) is a 6,900-frame /
230-second H.264 movie with audio. Treat these as style and pilot sources.
Native 4K detail requires new 2160 × 3840 renders per panel; scaling an
existing delivery does not create that detail. The different durations
also mean these movies cannot simply be assigned to different screens and
expected to loop together.

The U2718Q supports 3840 × 2160 at 60 Hz, DP 1.2 and **Mini DisplayPort**
inputs, HDMI, and VESA 100 × 100 mm mounting. “Micro DisplayPort” in the
brief is interpreted as Mini DisplayPort. Both DP connectors are inputs;
there is no DP output for monitor-to-monitor daisy chaining. Its USB hub
does not provide the smart-TV USB movie player in the historical setup.
[Dell U2718Q manual, pp. 7–15][dell]

Prefer native DP or USB-C **with DisplayPort Alt Mode/Thunderbolt video**
to DP. A USB-C connector alone does not establish video capability. The
Pi 5 and BrightSign alternatives output HDMI, so satisfying the DP-input
requirement requires **powered, active HDMI-source → DP-display converters**.
An ordinary DP-to-HDMI cable works in the wrong direction. One documented
candidate is StarTech **128-HDMI-DISPLAYPORT**, which supports 3840 × 2160
at 60 Hz and uses USB power. Its older **HD2DP** model is limited to 4K30.
Converter compatibility and timing must be checked on the actual chain.
[4K60 converter datasheet][converter]; [older 4K30 model][converter-old]

### What “frame accurate” means here

There are three separate requirements:

1. **Content alignment:** all channels present the corresponding source
   frame, including at startup and the loop boundary.
2. **Output timing alignment:** each video link begins its refresh at the
   same phase. This is the job of frame lock; genlock ties output timing to
   a reference. Shared software clocks alone do not establish it.
3. **Visible panel alignment:** the monitors display those signals with
   matching processing delay and panel response. Matching models help,
   but Dell does not specify an inter-panel synchronization guarantee.

NVIDIA distinguishes synchronized scan timing from the application swap
barrier that coordinates presentation of rendered frames. A hardware sync
board therefore needs suitable presentation software as well as correctly
configured outputs. [RTX PRO Sync user guide, terminology][nvidia-guide]

At 30 fps, a source frame lasts **33.333 ms**; at 60 Hz, a refresh lasts
**16.667 ms**. A 30 fps movie normally repeats each source frame twice at
60 Hz. Two outputs can have the same nominal refresh rate and still have
different refresh phases. Their clocks can also differ slightly in rate.
As an illustrative calculation, **100 ppm relative clock error produces
0.36 seconds of drift per hour**, about 11 source frames at 30 fps. This is
an example, not a measured Dell or Pi specification.

Starting three independent players together, sending a simultaneous UDP
“play,” or periodically restarting them only addresses part of the problem.
Continuous clock correction and a shared timeline prevent accumulating
content drift; synchronized output hardware addresses refresh timing.

**Most accurate achievable design:** shared frame selection, synchronized
buffer presentation, and hardware-locked scanout, followed by measurement
of the actual panels. This can align source frames and electrical raster
timing far more closely than one refresh. No defensible microsecond or
zero-visible-skew guarantee can be made for these U2718Qs from their
published specifications. Their 5/8 ms response-time rating is not a
measurement of input lag or inter-screen synchronization. If a contractual
visible-timing guarantee is necessary, specify and test the complete
display system, potentially including different panels. [Dell manual][dell]

### Five hardware/software options

Amounts below are **incremental USD planning allowances**, excluding the
three owned monitors, owned NUC/Pi, mounting, tax, shipping, and engineering
labor. Explicit advertised prices are distinguished from estimates; used
equipment requires a fresh quote and compatibility check.

| Option | Hardware and software | Resolution delivered to each panel | Synchronization assessment | Playback budget |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Existing NUC, one playback application | Native GPU outputs; GStreamer application, or licensed TouchDesigner | 4K60 if the exact NUC qualifies; otherwise test 1080p60 | Shared content timeline; output phase lock unproven | $40–250 connections with free software; add $600 for TouchDesigner Commercial |
| 2. Three Pi 5 players | Existing Pi + two more; wired switch; custom GStreamer controller; three HDMI→DP converters | Target 4K30 media over 4K60 links, one channel per Pi | Continuous network-clock correction; no hardware scanout lock | About $450–750; custom development extra |
| 3. One player + Datapath Fx4/D | NUC or Pi; single packed movie; Wall Designer; three DP outputs | 1920 × 1080 at 60 Hz signal, physically portrait and panel-scaled | One source frame, hardware-genlocked outputs; panel timing still tested | About $2,950–3,150 new including connections |
| 4. BrightSign XC4055 appliance | BrightSignOS multiscreen presentation; storage; three HDMI→DP converters | Three native 4K outputs | Vendor synchronization features; verify through converters and panels | About $2,100–2,400 |
| 5. Workstation + hardware synchronization | Compatible professional GPU + RTX PRO Sync / Quadro Sync II; Mosaic and suitable player | Three native 4K60 DP outputs | Strongest explicitly controlled frame and scan timing of these designs | Allow $1,500–4,000 for a used/refurbished build before paid playback software; quotation required |

#### 1. Existing NUC: recommended first experiment

Connect all three monitors to the **same GPU**, preferably through separate
native DP paths. Verify that the exact model supports three outputs at the
chosen resolution and refresh. A DP MST hub creates additional connections
within the upstream bandwidth and GPU limits; it does not create genlock.
Do not assume one DP 1.2 link can carry three uncompressed 4K60 RGB images.
For example, Intel's NUC8i3/5/7BE specification permits three 4K displays
using HDMI plus DP MST, but limits the two MST displays to 30 Hz. “Three
4K displays” is therefore insufficient procurement language.
[Intel NUC8 technical product specification, §1.5.1.5][nuc-example]

For a free Linux implementation, use one **GStreamer application** with a
shared clock/base time and coordinated channel readiness. A packed movie
decoded once and mapped to three outputs reduces independent decoder and
loop state. With three files, select the same frame index for all channels
and coordinate loop transitions. GStreamer's clock/timestamp model provides
the scheduling foundation; the wall application and its frame-presentation
policy still need to be implemented. [GStreamer clocks and synchronization][gst-clocks]

For Windows/macOS, **TouchDesigner Commercial** is a practical authoring
alternative: index the channels from one timeline, crop/map them to three
outputs, and arrange automatic startup. Check the NUC against current
system requirements and benchmark its decoder/GPU. The free Non-Commercial
edition has a **1280 × 1280** processing limit and cannot deliver these
full-resolution portrait frames. Commercial is listed at **$600**;
TouchDesigner's hardware frame-lock features require **Pro, $2,200**.
[Derivative licensing comparison][td-license]

An **mpv** instance spanning the wall can be a simpler pilot if a suitable
single wall movie decodes successfully and the desktop/window dimensions
are supported. `--loop-file=inf` and `--hwdec=auto` are useful starting
options; they do not synchronize separate players or output clocks.
An unusually tall movie may fail hardware decoding, even where ordinary
4K playback works. [mpv reference manual][mpv]

**Tradeoff:** lowest additional hardware cost and no distributed clock
problem, but neither a normal extended desktop nor one application proves
that the three output heads refresh together. Accept only after measuring
seam behavior. Avoid a USB DisplayLink graphics adapter as the default way
to add the third timing-critical output; qualify a native GPU path first.

#### 2. Three Pi 5s: inexpensive distributed experiment

Use one Pi per monitor, local media storage, cooling, reliable power,
wired Ethernet, and the same OS/player build. The Pi 5 exposes **two
micro-HDMI outputs** and advertises **4K60 HEVC decoding**; those facts do
not imply three outputs or three simultaneous 4K decodes. Do not plan a
single Pi 5 as the native three-screen player. Pre-rotate each channel into
a conventional **3840 × 2160 HEVC** file so the installation does not depend
on arbitrary tall decoder dimensions or realtime portrait rotation.
[Raspberry Pi display capabilities][pi-displays]; [BCM2712 decoder documentation][pi-chip]

Implement a controller that preloads every channel, waits for readiness,
and announces a future start epoch. Use a continuously calibrated
`GstNetClientClock`, or `GstPtpClock` with a configured PTP time provider,
and the **same base time, frame timestamps, and loop epoch** on all nodes.
A synchronized clock without this playback state is insufficient. Correct
late frames against the shared timeline instead of allowing each player
to free-run. This is a custom integration, not a `gst-launch` command or a
ready-made gallery appliance. [GstNetClientClock][gst-net]; [GstPtpClock][gst-ptp]

Budget example: two **2 GB Pi 5 boards at the October published $77.50 each**
are $155, plus roughly $100–180 for their power/cooling/storage, $120–270
for three suitable converters, and $50–100 for network/video connections.
Allow approximately $450–750 overall, including incidentals. This updates
the older $50–60 Pi price assumptions. [Raspberry Pi October 1 price notice][pi-price]

**Tradeoff:** attractive hardware cost and distributed decode capacity,
but substantial software work. Network clock synchronization does not
discipline the Pi's HDMI pixel clock automatically. Repeat/drop corrections
may remain visible at seams. Do not describe this design as guaranteed
frame lock; its measured performance may be adequate for some imagery.
Using the NUC for two panels and the owned Pi for the third could cost
less, but introduces the same distributed-clock work with unlike players.
It is a prototype fallback, not the preferred exhibition configuration.

#### 3. Datapath Fx4/D: synchronized outputs with reduced detail

Send **one 3840 × 2160 packed movie** from the NUC or Pi to an **Fx4/D**,
the DisplayPort-output version. Pack three pre-rotated 1920 × 1080 channel
images into three quadrants; leave the fourth black. Map those regions
to three 1920 × 1080 DP outputs with Wall Designer. Physical portrait
mounting turns each into a 1080 × 1920 picture, scaled by the Dell.
All output regions then originate from the same decoded source frame.

Datapath explicitly specifies **four genlocked outputs**, with DP outputs
rated for 1080p60/1200p60 or equivalent timings up to its pixel-rate limit.
This is **not three native 4K outputs**. The “4K” input and “8K × 8K” input
surface language do not change that. [Datapath Fx4 specifications][fx4]

The advertised new Fx4/D price found was **$2,850**, before cables and
storage. Rental or a tested used unit may make more sense for a short
exhibition; no current used bargain is assumed. [Fx4/D seller listing][fx4-price]

**Tradeoff:** simple single-source playback and explicitly synchronized DP
outputs, but poor value if native 4K detail is essential. This is a strong
fit for the existing 1080 × 1920 project deliveries. With a Pi source, use
hardware-decoded HEVC for the packed UHD movie and test the chosen input
format. A cheap generic “4K video wall controller” should not be substituted
without verifying per-output resolution, mapping, and output lock.

#### 4. BrightSign XC4055: compact unattended appliance

Use **one XC4055**, three outputs, local media storage, and three matched
active HDMI→DP converters. The XC4055 provides **four HDMI outputs, quad
4K60 decoding, and 4K video rotation**. The XC2055 has only two outputs and
is not a direct substitute for this three-panel wall. [BrightSign XC5 datasheet][xc5]

Build a multiscreen presentation with identical panel orientations,
resolutions, and exact frame rates. Configure synchronized zones when
using separate channel files, rather than three independent playlists.
BrightSign documents this authoring workflow and its output/codec limits.
Use local publication for a fixed exhibition, verifying the selected
authoring workflow's licensing; a cloud content-management subscription
should not be assumed necessary for playback. [Multiscreen authoring guide][bright-multiscreen]

BrightSign describes three cooperating mechanisms: **Jam Sync** aligns
starts, **Frame Sync** corrects playback continuously, and **HDMI Clock
Sync** aligns link timing. Those are vendor capabilities, not measurements
of this particular DP-converted installation. Confirm their applicability
to the selected XC4055 presentation/OS configuration and test the complete
path. [BrightSign video sync documentation][bright-sync]

A seller listed the XC4055 at **$1,865**, temporarily out of stock at the
time of research. Allow about $2,100–2,400 with converters, storage, and
cables; availability needs reconfirmation. [XC4055 seller listing][xc5-price]

If one appliance cannot be sourced, three matching 4K-capable BrightSign
players are an alternative within this option. Their documented wall
architecture uses Ethernet/PTP and common playback state. Seamless files
can remain synchronized through loops; otherwise the leader must explicitly
resynchronize each loop. This is more complete than a generic UDP start.
[BrightSign wall synchronization architecture][bright-architecture]

**Tradeoff:** likely less maintenance than a custom Pi application, but
converters add cost and another timing/compatibility dependency. If DP is
only a preference and can later be relaxed, direct HDMI to the Dells is
an alternative that removes those converters; it is not assumed in this
proposal's DP-compliant budget.

#### 5. Professional GPU and synchronization board: precision route

Use a workstation with a **compatible GPU and sync board**, three direct
DP cables, matching output timings, and a single mapped wall presentation.
An **RTX A4000 + Quadro Sync II / compatible RTX PRO Sync board** is one
documented hardware family: the A4000 has four DP outputs and lists Sync II
compatibility. Match the exact board revision, GPU, driver, and firmware
using the current compatibility list. This needs a suitable chassis and
slots; it is not a graphics-card upgrade for an ordinary tiny NUC.
[RTX A4000 datasheet][a4000]; [current RTX PRO Sync compatibility][nvidia-sync]

Configure frame lock and a Mosaic wall or an application with appropriate
synchronized buffer swaps. Hardware lock without coordinated content
selection can still show different source frames. If TouchDesigner is the
presentation application, include its **Pro** license for hardware lock;
otherwise qualify another player or a custom application against the
chosen presentation API. [NVIDIA sync user guide][nvidia-guide];
[Derivative feature matrix][td-license]

Allow **$1,500–4,000** for a used/refurbished workstation, compatible GPU,
sync board, storage, and cables, before paid playback software. This is a
planning range, not a verified current bundle price. A legacy
**Quadro P4000 + Sync II** is another documented combination to price, but
older driver support and decode throughput need separate qualification.
Do not buy a low-profile four-output card on the assumption that four
connectors imply sync-board compatibility. [P4000 hardware datasheet][p4000]

**Tradeoff:** best route here to explicitly verified electrical frame lock
at native resolution, with more space, setup effort, and potential cost.
An external house-sync generator is optional for a standalone wall; it is
needed only if the wall must follow an external timing reference. Visible
panel timing remains a commissioning measurement.

### Content preparation for an image that crosses all three panels

Author one continuous coordinate space and one frame sequence, then cut
it into channels. Replaying unrelated existing loops in sync would not
create a continuous top-to-bottom image.

- For a native wall, top = `y:[0,3840)`, middle = `y:[3840,7680)`, bottom =
  `y:[7680,11520)` in a 2160-pixel-wide master. Use common simulation time
  and seeds before cropping, so effects cross the channel boundaries.
- Measure the two physical seam gaps. For window-like continuity, model
  those gaps as hidden picture area; otherwise deliberately use contiguous
  crops with a visible jump over each bezel. Apply compensation once.
- Avoid assuming a **2160 × 11520 encoded movie** will hardware-decode.
  Keep the tall master as a rendering coordinate system. Deliver three
  conventional **3840 × 2160 pre-rotated files**, or pack those rotated
  channels into three quadrants of a **7680 × 4320 HEVC atlas** when the
  selected player supports that decode size and region mapping. A fourth
  quadrant is unused. The atlas is an engineering proposal requiring a
  decoder/mapping trial, not an automatic capability of every player.
- For the pilot/Fx4, use 1920 × 1080 rotated channel files or a
  **3840 × 2160 atlas**. This preserves the existing deliveries' picture
  resolution while giving all channels one source frame in the atlas case.
- Give every channel exactly the same frame rate, frame count, timestamp
  origin, and loop duration. At 30 fps a 180-second loop has **5,400 frames**.
  Use integer frame indexing and exact rational frame rates; distinguish
  30/60 from 30000/1001 and 60000/1001. Test loop-end decoder behavior.
- Use HEVC at native 4K where hardware support is confirmed. Benchmark
  8-bit and 10-bit delivery on the chosen system; do not assume that the
  existing 10-bit HEVC encode is optimal for every player. Keep dense moiré
  linework in the quality test, since scaling/compression changes the art.
- If audio is retained, use one playback authority and one speaker feed.
  Do not let three independent audio devices become separate timing masters.

For a shared-clock implementation, the intended selection rule is
`frame = floor((shared_time - start_epoch) * fps) mod frame_count`.
Keep loop scheduling on that same continuous clock. Decode/preroll ahead
of deadlines; prevent one channel from advancing independently when another
is late. Adopt an explicit coordinated hold, resync, or restart policy for
underruns. These are application requirements, not features supplied by
matching the file durations alone.

### Mounting and unattended operation

Each standless Dell is **3.70 kg**, with a **611.3 × 356.2 mm** enclosure
in landscape; the portrait stack is approximately **1.834 m high × 0.356 m
wide**, before gaps, and weighs **11.1 kg** before mounts/cables. Three
separately positioned low-profile VESA wall mounts on a properly supported
backing are a practical choice. A freestanding alternative needs explicit
support for three portrait 27-inch panels and at least 611 mm vertical
center spacing plus gaps. Many “triple vertical” desk poles are sized for
landscape monitors. Dell specifies **M4 × 10 mm** mounting screws; follow
the mount's spacer and engagement instructions. Budget a separate estimated
$100–300 for basic wall-mount hardware, excluding structural work.
[Dell mounting and physical specifications, pp. 15 and 30–31][dell]

For exhibition operation, specify automatic power-on after AC restoration,
player startup without login interaction, disabled sleep/screensavers,
fixed display mapping and refresh, a restart watchdog, and local media
with checksums. Record the mapping from output/adapter to panel number.
Match monitor presets, brightness, response mode, and SDR/color settings;
avoid automatic image adjustments. Keep software updates outside show
hours, retain a known-working image/configuration, and provide a simple
staff restart procedure. An optional UPS should be sized from measured
total load. A distributed player must rejoin the **current** wall position,
not start its own loop at frame zero after a reboot.

### Commissioning and decision gates

1. **Inventory the NUC before buying accessories.** Record model, OS,
   GPU/driver, physical port types and routing, supported simultaneous
   timings, and hardware decoding of the intended files. Check all three
   connected panels at once. Test 4K60 and, if necessary, an explicitly
   labeled 1080p60 pilot. Do not infer these from the machine running this
   research session.
2. **Create a diagnostic loop.** Put the same source-frame number and
   alternating flash marker on every channel, plus a moving shape crossing
   both seams. Include representative dense moiré passages and marks on
   the final/first loop frames. Repeat enough to expose loop transitions.
3. **Measure the image, not only player logs.** Film all panels together
   with a short exposure at 240 fps or faster: 240 fps samples approximately
   every **4.17 ms**, so it cannot certify microsecond synchronization.
   Compare equivalent scan positions; portrait rotation and camera rolling
   shutter can otherwise mislead. Use photodiodes/oscilloscope measurements
   if tighter timing certification is required.
4. **Run at least eight hours**, inspecting startup, intermediate points,
   every sampled loop seam, and the end. Record frame-number differences,
   transition skew, drops/repeats, black gaps, and whether skew changes over
   time. Test a player reboot, monitor power cycle, and network interruption
   where applicable. Recovery is part of the acceptance test.
5. **Apply two distinct acceptance levels.** For the inexpensive pilot,
   propose visible transition skew no greater than one 60 Hz refresh
   (**16.67 ms**), no cumulative drift, no independent loop slip, and an
   artistic review of seam crossings. This is a test target, not a promised
   property. For the precision build, require corresponding source frames,
   verified hardware-lock status, and a measured maximum panel skew agreed
   for the work; record any instrument-resolution limit.

**Purchase decision:** spend first on qualifying the owned NUC and one
representative three-channel test. If it passes the artistic timing target,
it is the cheapest proposed solution. If it lacks three suitable outputs,
price a used three-output mini PC/workstation before building a Pi cluster.
Choose the Fx4/D only when 1080 × 1920 detail per panel is acceptable.
Choose the XC4055 for compact appliance operation after converter testing.
Choose a compatible professional GPU/sync-board workstation when native
4K and verifiable scan timing outweigh size and setup cost. There is no
researched basis for promising strict three-screen frame lock from the
owned Pi 5 alone or from three independent USB loop players.
