## 1. Image analysis in the corrected orientation

After the 180° turn, the large **50** is upright. The paragraph occupies the
left cartouche, the serial occupies the right cartouche, matching calligraphic
monograms sit below the paired scrolls, and the location/date inscription is
under the central ornament. All descriptions and future measurements in this
plan use that reading orientation.

The design concentrates elaborate sepia linework in a central medallion and
a broad, approximately bilateral frame. Large areas of pale paper remain
open inside the side cartouches. Its characteristic contrast comes from
alternating brown outlines on paper and fine pale filaments within denser
brown regions. Preserve that visual polarity as carefully as curve shape.

### Central medallion

A small floral web forms the center. Several nested scalloped bands surround
it, each with its own loop rhythm and a clearly defined boundary. Fine strands
cross within the bands; narrow pale gutters and dark contour lines separate
neighboring bands. The outer rings are not simply enlarged copies of one
rosette: their widths, scallop depths, and filament directions differ.

The large serif `50` overlays the inner bands. Its pale stroke shapes and
fine dark edging interrupt the engraving, while the counters retain visible
background structure. Matching those counter openings, serif shapes, and
overlap boundaries is necessary for a convincing reconstruction.

### Outer crown

Eight principal protrusions surround the medallion: four larger motifs on
the cardinal axes and four smaller diagonal motifs. The larger forms have
pointed, lobed envelopes and dense fan-like webs; the diagonal forms have
rounded, angular envelopes with crossing strands and small dark wedges.
Their eight-part arrangement is visually clear, but their internal crossing
counts and the central rings' scallop counts still require measurement.

The crown sits behind the concentric medallion. Thin light separations and
dark outlines distinguish the overlapping components. Model these joins and
boundaries explicitly so added line density does not flatten the ornament
into one dark disk.

### Scroll frame and ribbons

Four sweeping, tapered ribbon regions connect the vicinity of the crown to
the outer side frame. They carry a much smaller repeating loop texture and
have several closely spaced outline curves. Their long, shallow arcs and
pointed ends are important to the overall silhouette.

Each side cartouche has a broad rounded outer boundary and a pair of inward
spirals near its lower end. Those scrolls have visible thick-to-thin strokes,
clean tapered terminals, and substantial open paper between their turns.
The two matching monograms below them are calligraphic compositions with
large flourishes and fine hairlines, not small guilloche rosettes.

### Text, paper, and uncertainty

The paragraph, monograms, and date line use flowing script. The serial is
darker and typographically distinct. The `50` is identifiable; a full exact
transcription of the smaller text and identification of every monogram letter
are not established by this planning pass. Preserve observed letter shapes
without inventing uncertain wording.

Paper tone, edge curvature, uneven lighting, faint show-through, and marks
belong to the photographed presentation. In particular, the broad gray
shading is not evidence for a deliberately shaded guilloche vignette. The
source does not establish physical paper dimensions or an original ink recipe.
Treat the proposed curve equations as fitted models, not a recovered account
of the historical production process.

## 4. Geometry and izzi enhancement plan

### 4.1 Measure before fixing defaults

Create a new `examples.data/guilloche-dutch-stage7/reference.json` containing
the source hash, orientation, crop, transforms, normalized landmarks, palette
observations, and uncertainty. Measure the medallion center and radii, each
scalloped boundary, crown tips, ribbon boundaries and endpoints, side-cartouche
extents, scroll centers, monogram boxes, and text baselines.

Fit large shapes first. Use symmetry to initialize the frame and crown, then
record visible deviations supported by the corrected image. Do not mistake
perspective or lighting differences for intentionally different left/right
geometry. Reserve at least one unfit patch per texture type as a check against
matching only the regions used for calibration.

Define nine detail regions: `core-and-50`, `scalloped-bands`, `cardinal-petal`,
`diagonal-petal`, `ribbon-weave`, `scroll-terminal`, `monogram`,
`script-and-date`, and `serial`. The script/date record can contain separate
subregions. Measure pale-line width, dark-line width, gap width, and pitch
independently; image darkness alone does not identify the correct geometry.

### 4.2 Woven bands on scalloped carriers

Compare two small native studies: an existing elliptic loop with a bounded
radial deformation, and loops evaluated directly in the tangent/normal frame
of a scalloped carrier. The latter is the proposed reusable addition,
`contour_loop_spec`, using a small set of existing `contour_mode` values:

```text
R(t) = r * (1 + sum(a_m * cos(m*t + phi_m)))
C(t) = origin + R(t) * (cos(t), sin(t))
T(t) = C'(t) / |C'(t)|
N(t) = (T_y(t), -T_x(t))
q    = k*t + loop_phase
P(t) = C(t) + A_n * (cos(q) + secondary*cos(2*q)) * N(t)
            + A_t * sin(q) * T(t)
```

This definition uses SVG's downward-positive y axis. Document conversion to
the existing polar-contour convention and test an asymmetric, nonzero-phase
fixture to catch accidental mirroring. Carrier scalloping and fine loop phase
must be independent controls: moving the weave must not rotate every boundary.

Use positive, regular carriers, bounded mode amplitudes, integer closed-loop
cycles, and exact repeated endpoints. Select sampling from both carrier and
filament frequencies, then verify final-scale chord error. Intentional loop
crossings are valid; a degenerate carrier tangent is not.

Build each annular band from measured inner/outer contours, its own filament
family, separator gutters, and outlines. Permit per-band phase, width, and
frequency; verify clear gaps at both peaks and troughs. Fit the floral center
with the existing rational roulette/rose primitives. Freeze the measured
ring and scallop counts only after inspecting the detail regions.

### 4.3 Crown motifs

Construct one major petal and one diagonal motif in local coordinates before
repeating them. Fit their boundaries with existing contour modes where
possible, or with a small explicit cubic outline. Fill them with clipped,
phase-shifted roulette or loop families, keeping their focal fans, central
crossings, narrow light gaps, and dark wedges separately identifiable.

Start with four major motifs at 90° intervals and four smaller motifs offset
by 45°. Fit the measured radii, envelope proportions, and overlap depths.
Apply transforms to geometry and its clipping region together. The medallion
must occlude the bases of the petals cleanly without erasing their outer rims.

### 4.4 Tapered ribbons and cartouche scrolls

Author the four ribbon outlines as paired smooth cubic boundaries with matched
endpoints. Test a regular native micro-loop lattice clipped into each ribbon
first. If its rhythm fails to follow the photographed arcs, map the lattice
along a ribbon centerline parameterized by arc length:

```text
B(s, v) = C(s) + v * h(s) * N(s),   -1 <= v <= 1
```

Here `h(s)` is the measured half-width and `s` is distance along the curve.
Spacing controls must have drawing-unit meanings. Compare fixed-size cells
clipped at the taper with cells gently scaled across the width; select the
behavior supported by the reference. Avoid crushing every cell to an ink
blob at the tip. Require a regular centerline and a nonfolding strip interior;
zero width is allowed only at declared tapered endpoints.

Keep the fitted ribbon outlines in the example. Promote the mapping/fill to
a bounded `ribbon_loop_field_spec` only if the study demonstrates that existing
clipping and repeat controls cannot express it. This is a conditional addition,
not a dependency on a general-purpose path-deformation framework.

Fit each cartouche outline and its paired curls as connected cubic segments
or a spiral with explicit entry tangent and growth controls. Preserve the
large open centers. Use filled stroke outlines where thick-to-thin taper is
visible; a uniform-width spiral loses the calligraphic character. Reuse common
geometry across the two sides while preserving separately measured lettering.

### 4.5 Paint polarity, masks, and typography

Assign distinct roles to paper, sepia ornament, darker serial, and diagnostic
overlays. The central bands appear as pale strands over brown regions; the
side flourishes appear as brown strokes on pale paper. Implement pale strands
as controlled knockouts or masks exposing the underlying paper when that
matches the reference. Do not infer a separate physical white ink from the
photograph. Transparent/gradient paper tests should confirm that a knockout
actually reveals the ground rather than leaving an opaque pale patch.

Initial paint order: paper; ribbon/frame ground and microtexture; crown
motifs; scalloped band grounds, filaments, gutters, and outlines; core;
outlined `50`; script, serial, and monograms; optional capture treatment.
Adjust local ordering from the overlap evidence. Keep stable IDs for every
ring, petal, ribbon, scroll, text region, clip, and mask.

Use reference-specific vector outlines for `50`, preserving its counters
and dark edging. Compare a suitable pinned font with the photograph before
using it for script or serial text; convert final glyphs to paths. For the
monograms and unusual script flourishes, a carefully cleaned local trace may
give better fidelity. Their sepia color overlaps the frame's color, so the
stage-6 black-ink extraction thresholds are not reusable defaults. Constrain
tracing to measured regions and inspect counters, hairlines, and joins.

Store a confidence-marked transcription separately from the vector shapes.
Unresolved characters remain recorded uncertainties. Pin new source-specific
assets with hashes and simplification tolerances; keep the generator independent
of the JPEG, host fonts, and tracing libraries.

#### Script font deliverable — added at user request

Build a usable OpenType/CFF `.otf` font from the paragraph/date script and
include it in stage 7. Font family name: **C-A-Mechelse-1945** (specified by the user after the initial delivery).
Map confidently identified source glyphs to Unicode, normalize baseline,
x-height, scale, and spacing, and preserve the source's slant and stroke
contrast. Treat the monograms as separate decorative artwork rather than
assuming their flourishes identify ordinary alphabetic characters.

Include the generated OTF, editable glyph sources, a deterministic fontTools
build script, a character-coverage/provenance manifest, and SVG/PNG specimen
sheets. The manifest must distinguish traced characters, reconstructed
characters, and unsupported characters. Do not claim recovery of an original
historical typeface or a complete alphabet when the image does not supply one.
Test CFF structure, Unicode mappings, advances, counter winding, font loading,
and an actual rendered specimen. Verify that the artwork remains independent
of an installed font by retaining its pinned vector lettering.

Initial scope refinement (superseded by the full-font request in section 10):
the initial font contains 17 Unicode mappings
(space, `A M a d e i m r s t`, `1 4 5 7 9`, and period) and connected
`Amsterdam`/`Mei` ligatures. The word ligatures are cleaned source traces.
Individual reusable characters are smooth source-guided reconstructions:
reliably separating joined date letters from the photograph did not yield

### 4.6 API boundaries, serialization, and budgets

Proposed assembly API:
`dutch_scroll_medallion_tuning` and
`make_dutch_scroll_medallion(origin, width, height, ink_plate, tuning)`.
Return named native compositions for the medallion, crown, and patterned
frame. Expose measured ring controls, petal proportions, ribbon pitch, frame
extent, and component visibility. Keep typography and capture effects in the
example's presentation layer. Finalize the API after the motif studies.

Add any new reusable specs to `src/izzi-svg-guilloche.h` without changing
existing stage-6 defaults or sampling paths. Update all affected visitors:
validation, point estimates, sampling, closure, JSON read/write, and profile
conversion. Implement strict geometry selectors such as `contour_loop` in
`src/izzi-svg-guilloche-json.h` and the scene schema. Preserve all controls
in resolved parameter records and reject unknown members. A `pattern.family`

Use a frequency-aware sampling floor and target at most 0.25 pixel chord error

assets, and example-level transformations as well as core scene estimates.
Reject non-finite parameters, invalid band ordering, resource overflow, and

rather than embedding eleven full native scenes into one oversized SVG.

## 8. Fidelity targets, validation, and completion

Evaluate fidelity in order: orientation and layout; silhouettes and band
topology; crossing rhythm and spacing; ink/paper polarity; stroke taper;
typography; then capture appearance. Keep successive fits and a written list
of remaining discrepancies. Raw pixel error against the upside-down,
unrectified JPEG cannot establish geometric accuracy.

Proposed acceptance targets, to be measured during implementation:

- Correct 180° orientation with no reflection: paragraph left, serial right,
  upright `50`, and date below. Check forward/inverse transform consistency.
- Major centers and envelope landmarks within 1% of panel dimensions after
  registration, with a correctly paired four-plus-four faithful crown.
- Ring topology, band ordering, numeral counters, and intended crossing
  relationships match direct inspection. Scallop and ring counts are recorded
  measurements rather than arbitrary defaults.
- Median pitch, filament width, and separator-gap differences within 10% in
  clear calibrated and held-out texture patches, with the coverage and
  measurement uncertainty stated. Report each patch and each polarity
  separately so one successful region cannot hide another's failure.
- Curve chord error at most 0.25 pixel at final export scale; no visible
  closure seam, broken scroll join, band leakage, plugged glyph counter,
  vanished hairline, or collapsed ribbon tip at 100% and enlarged scale.

These are targets, not current results. Revise a target only with an explicit
reason and retained prior measurement. Report a visually approximate candidate
