# De Kosnik — 24th anniversary guilloche bills

Date: 2026-11-08. Status: **approved; digital implementation delivered**. Scope: **ten distinct
two-sided cards, twenty designed faces**, with photographic portraits translated
into drypoint or line-photogravure-style marks. The user approved implementation
with “approve anniversary plan, resume unimplemented implementation”. All twenty
faces are now rendered; physical proofing and selection remain pending.
See [execution and delivery](#12-execution-and-delivery) for the implemented
commands, artifacts and remaining print-dependent decisions. Sections 1–11
retain the approved design brief and initial production targets.

The cards should feel like intimate, highly ornate ceremonial affidavits:
recognizable portraits held within elaborate scrolls, woven borders, scalloped
seals and fine interference fields. Every front and every back carries the
exact text **De Kosnik**, the number **24**, and the year **2026**. Each reverse
is a complementary composition with its own portrait treatment and a quieter
space for a short declaration.

## 2. Physical format and common composition

Adopt the published U.S. note dimensions, **6.14 × 2.61 inches**, as the trim
size: **155.956 × 66.294 mm**, landscape. The dimensional reference is the
[U.S. Currency Education Program](https://www.uscurrency.gov/history/archived).

| Print review | Actual size, with fit-to-page disabled; separate magnified detail sheets |

Stage 7's existing panel is 1000 × 444.850891. Recompose its vertical spacing
for the bill format. Preserve circular medallions and portrait proportions;
adjust ribbon reach, cartouche position and border spacing rather than scaling
the complete reference panel unequally in x and y.

On the front, a portrait field occupies roughly 45–65% of the usable width,
with a prominent `24` seal and `De Kosnik` as the principal inscription. On
the reverse, retain a recognizable portrait or paired portrait vignettes,
move the strongest ornament toward the border, and leave a readable declaration
cartouche. The pair shares its palette, scroll vocabulary and edition ID.

Build each face in this order:

1. Paper and bounded pale-color fields.
2. Portrait engraving, including a restrained amount of its original setting.
3. Quiet surface-tension contours around the portrait silhouettes.
4. Local moiré panels in ribbons, margins or secondary seals.
5. Dominant guilloche medallions, scrolls, woven borders and corner ornaments.
6. Name, numeral, year and secondary text, with clean spaces behind lettering.

Every face uses guilloche, moiré and surface tension, with different prominence.
Keep eyes, mouths and important gestures clear of dense crossing patterns.
Use recorded clipping and exclusion shapes to achieve this. The portraits
remain structural background artwork, clearly legible through the ornament.

## 4. Portrait engraving method

Develop a deterministic image-to-path adapter; the existing guilloche and
field renderers do not themselves turn a photograph into an engraved portrait.
The primary deliverable is drawn line geometry with source-derived tone and
shape, suitable for composition with the existing native patterns.

**Drypoint treatment (D):** simplify the background, preserve the portrait's
major contours, and follow gently varying directions through hair, clothing
and facial shading. Build tone with tapered short strokes and a sparse second
hatch direction in the deepest shadows. Use small seeded deviations to vary
the marks; keep their direction and length stable around eyes and mouths.
An occasional fine companion stroke can suggest a drypoint burr. Avoid a blur
filter that turns the entire portrait into a soft dark patch.

**Line photogravure treatment (G):** sample the photograph along continuous
parallel or gently bowed carriers. Map normalized local luminance into line
width and intentional highlight gaps, with local shadow limits. Use one
dominant direction through a face and an optional second direction in clothing
or background only. This is a digital line interpretation, with tone carried
by the spacing and width of the paths.

For both methods, orient the image, set the crop, normalize tonal range, and
author a subject/feature mask before generating marks. Keep glasses, masks,
hair and expressions from the source; do not invent obscured facial details.
Crop or simplify distracting scenery deliberately and record that decision.
Mirror ornament freely where the composition calls for it; preserve the
photographs' orientation.

Initial physical proof ranges, to be adjusted after printing:

| Mark | Starting range at final card size |
| --- | --- |
| Portrait strokes | 0.10–0.18 mm; sparse 0.08 mm hairlines as proof candidates |
| Portrait carrier pitch | 0.30–0.55 mm; width modulation always leaves a visible gap |
| Guilloche filaments | 0.10–0.16 mm; bolder separator outlines 0.18–0.25 mm |
| Secondary moiré/surface contours | 0.08–0.13 mm, with 0.10 mm as the first production candidate |
| Moiré pitch and angle separation | Begin around 0.35–0.60 mm and 2–6 degrees; inspect intentional beats at actual size |
| Portrait shadow coverage | Start with a 35% local painted-area ceiling; relax only where likeness needs it |

These are proposed rendering parameters, not established capabilities of the
printer. Preserve enough native photo resolution to sample the chosen crop
at 600 dpi; assess the smaller P17 crop at its actual placed dimensions.
Keep portrait carrier directions separate from nearby moiré directions so
unintended interference does not replace facial detail.

“Light cobalt” is the project's design name for the chart's Pale/Blue swatch;
the chart itself does not label a cobalt color. Compare it with Pale/Azure
before selecting the preferred blue field. The plan uses both as print-proof
swatches and uses Pale/Blue for the initial cobalt card compositions.

Define four reusable recipes:

- **P — Peach:** bounded peach portrait field, brown portrait/name/primary
  scrolls, dark blue fine interlace, small lime accents.
- **L — Lime:** bounded lime field, brown portrait, dark blue seal and border,
  small peach cartouches. Strong readable lettering stays brown or dark blue.
- **C — Cobalt:** bounded pale-blue field, brown portrait and script, dark blue
  structural lines, small peach or cream counterfields. Keep blue away from
  large solid shadow masses.
- **W — Paper:** predominantly exposed paper, separate peach/lime/cobalt
  ribbons or lobes, with the same brown/blue line hierarchy.

As starting composition targets, leave at least 40% of a face as exposed paper,
limit dark brown/blue painted geometry to roughly 8–12% of the face, and build
ornament through repetition, intersections and negative space. Measure the
union of painted areas separately from pale-field area, so overlapping paths
are not simply added twice. These are review targets and relative coverage
proxies; they do not estimate cartridge consumption.

Use named colors at full opacity where possible. Make lighter portrait regions
by reducing line width or frequency; use explicit pale colors for fields.
Keep the broad dark backgrounds of some Stage 7 examples out of these initial
recipes. Blue is retained as requested, in thin outlines and light fields.
An RGB file cannot impose per-channel C/VM limits; any printer-side channel
control would be a separately configured and measured production setting.

## 9. Production sequence and review

1. **Freeze sources and type.** Save the 27-file inventory, selected crops,
   source hashes and explicit font paths. Make source/crop comparisons and
   a specimen containing `De Kosnik`, `24`, `2026`, `DK` and the short copy.
2. **Prove engraving and palette at card size.** Use P12, P18 and P17 to compare
   D/G treatments at three line pitches. Put peach, apricot, lime, cobalt,
   azure, brown and both dark blues beside the same eye/hair/cloth crop and
   fine guilloche sample. Include a stroke/gap ladder and 7.5–9 pt Atkinson
   text. Keep the actual-size proof compact enough for one sheet.
3. **Build V01, V03 and V08 front/back prototypes.** Establish portrait
   recognition, lettering hierarchy, pale-field extent and the balance of
   the three pattern families. Inspect actual-size and enlarged proofs.
4. **Produce all ten paired compositions.** Carry the successful portrait
   controls and print geometry into the other seven designs, keeping their
   authored composition differences. Deliver twenty complete faces together
   for comparison, with proposed choices visibly separated from selected ones.
5. **Physical proof and refinement.** Record the actual P9000 media/profile,
   color-management path, nozzle-check condition and paper. Compare cobalt
   against azure, inspect fine dark-blue marks, and revise density or areas
   where needed. Evaluate likeness and ornament by eye at 100% size. Digital
   coverage and color values alone cannot establish the printed result.
6. **Prepare the chosen print set.** Confirm stock suitable for both sides,
   then use a numbered duplex calibration sheet. Record front/back placement,
   feed orientation and measured registration before imposing the final set.
   Target registration within 0.5 mm and keep key details clear of the trim.

The first ten digital pairs can be completed using the proposed recipes before
the physical proof. Record print-dependent choices as provisional. Do not
silently interpret a successful export as selection of a favorite design.
