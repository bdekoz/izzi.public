# Buckminster Fuller’s World Game: a multivariable atlas, geometric language, and working environment

2026-09-20 · Expanded archive examination and implications for Izzi, cartofreako, and Alpha60.

The expanded photographs support a substantial revision of the pilot’s emphasis. **“Oblique matrix” describes one layout operation, but it is inadequate as the organizing description of this material.** The World Chart combines geographic hierarchy, periods, subjects, multiple quantities within each record, normalization, and trends. Around it are geographic resource overlays, distribution networks, polyhedral constructions, packing studies, circular comparison glyphs, and artwork for film sequences. Together these form a system for moving between quantities, places, relationships, scales, and possible futures.

The most useful contemporary extension is therefore a family of coordinated representations with explicit data semantics. Izzi can supply reusable glyphs and geometric constructions; cartofreako can place them in geographic context; Alpha60 can supply scoped swarm observations and aggregation. Much of the foundation already exists. In particular, Izzi already implements a paired-triangle count/share matrix, and cartofreako already renders all ten downloader network fields. The next additions should support richer records, paired uploader/downloader views, stable aggregation, and comparisons across time and scenarios.

## 1. What the expanded archive establishes

### Scope and provenance

The supplied archive directory contains **112 JPEG photographs and one PNG**. All JPEGs were screened in six filename-labeled contact sheets; selected originals were then examined individually for legends, annotations, geometry, and provenance. The PNG was also inspected. These are 113 files, not 113 distinct historical works: many photographs repeat a sheet, show a detail, or show its negative/positive counterpart. The capture filenames indicate March 2, 2026; they do not date the photographed objects.

The box-label photograph, `115224554`, identifies **M1378**, *Buckminster Fuller’s World Game, 1965–1980*, with **Herbert Matter** named on the label, Flat Box 7. Further photographs show Flat Boxes 8 and 9. Stanford’s finding aid for the separate R. Buckminster Fuller papers lists M1378 as related original graphic art, film, video, audio, manuscripts, and correspondence from Herbert and Mercedes Matter’s files. This is stronger identification than the pilot’s folder-based attribution, but it does not establish a box/folder/item citation or Fuller’s personal authorship for every photographed drawing. [Stanford finding aid, related collections](https://oac.cdlib.org/findaid/ark%3A%2F13030%2Ftf109n9832).

The handwritten film-credit sheet, `130333381`, names Saturn Pictures, Herbert Matter, Fuller, and Edwin Schlossberg, and refers to the New York Studio School, summer 1969. That is evidence about the production’s stated setting, not a creation date for all sheets. “Fuller/World Game graphics in the Matter collection” is consequently a more accurate attribution than “drawings by Fuller.”

This report distinguishes **observed** features, **interpretations**, and **proposals**. It does not recover a numerical archive dataset. Fine handwriting, glare, sleeves, folds, and photographed reproductions still limit transcription. No generated enhancement is used as evidence. The [September 15 pilot](20260915.buckminister_fuller_archive_1.md), including its later implementation notes, remains useful background; the following corrects and extends it.

### The decisive new evidence: the original World Chart key

Original paste-up of the World Chart key, with independently labeled quantities and a trend field

The paste-up key, `133120400`, makes eleven labels directly readable:

| Field family | Labels visible on the original | Interpretation boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Nutrition | Calories; grams of protein | Different measures, not interchangeable units or parts of a total. The key alone does not specify their time basis. |
| Per-person resource quantities | Per-capita energy slaves; per-capita metric tons, non-electric; per-capita kilowatt-hours | Three normalized quantities, each requiring its own definition and period. |
| Aggregate resource quantities | Total kilowatt-hours; total metric tons, non-electric; total energy slaves | Totals paired conceptually with their per-capita counterparts. |
| Population context | Population; density population | A denominator and a spatial intensity; density also requires a geographic area definition. |
| Change | Trend | A designated place for change information; the exact calculation is not established by the label. |

There are **ten quantity labels plus a trend field**, not eleven proven independent degrees of freedom. Population, totals, and per-capita values can be mathematically related. “Energy slaves” is the archive’s historical work-equivalent terminology; new data should use explicit physical units and a documented conversion if retaining that comparison.

The photographed chalkboard key, `111959892`, corroborates the arrangement of aggregate/per-capita measures, population, density, and trend. The color chart, `120213318`, shows regional and country bands, repeated years from 1965 to 2000, and subjects including life expectancy, mortality, arable land, housing, metals, food, communication, and power. It also shows colored and internally subdivided records that the earlier monochrome image made harder to interpret. Their exact color-to-variable and fill-to-magnitude rules remain unresolved.

### Correcting the dimensional characterization

Three different meanings of “dimension” must be kept distinct:

| Kind of dimension | What is present | What it means for a renderer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Data dimensions | Place, geographic hierarchy, period, subject, and multiple quantitative attributes | Preserve these as separate fields and indexing choices. A row/column address does not describe the complete record. |
| Drawing dimensions | A two-dimensional page with several line directions, text, color, subdivision, repetition, and overlays | Many variables can be encoded on a plane. Three directions in a triangular lattice still span only two spatial dimensions. |
| Represented spatial dimensions | Drawings of tetrahedra, octahedra, icosahedra, other solids, spheres, and nets | Some sheets represent genuine three-dimensional constructions; their projection onto paper is distinct from statistical dimensionality. |

The pilot’s affine placement equation remains valid for placing cell anchors. Its limitation is explanatory: moving a two-index table onto a slant does not account for the many fields inside each record, the mixed year/subject bands, the geographic overlays, or the geometric transformation studies.

A better description of the chart is a **multivariable resource atlas organized on a triangular lattice, with compound records and geographic and temporal indexing**. A better description of the broader corpus is a **visual system for resource accounting, geographic relationships, geometric explanation, and sequential presentation**. These are analytical descriptions, not recovered historical titles.

A suitable modern data model would be a sparse set of observations:

```text
observation = {
  entity_id, parent_entity_id, geography_version,
  period, subject_id, metric_id,
  value, unit, denominator_id,
  observation_status, transcription_status, source_reference
}

view = {
  selected_entities, selected_periods, selected_metrics,
  grouping, glyph_template, layout_or_projection, scale_definitions
}
```

Scenario can be an additional index when the source establishes one. This model does **not** claim that the sheet contains a fully populated place × year × subject cube. Some subjects occupy separate bands, and the distinction between measured, projected, and unfilled entries still requires documentary work. Likewise, a triangular appearance does not establish a ternary plot: calories, mass, population, and electricity are not three components summing to one.

### Connections across Fuller’s career

The strongest relationship is a repeated move between **local components and an intelligible whole**. A field belongs to a compound record; a record to a regional inventory; a triangular face to a world map; a structural member to a polyhedron; a frame to a sequence. This is an interpretation of the photographs, not proof that all these objects implement one mathematical scheme.

The Dymaxion sheets connect directly to Fuller and Shoji Sadao’s cartography, including the 1954 icosahedral map. A triangular map face is part of a globe’s unfolding; a triangular statistical cell is a record container. Shared geometry provides a visual relationship without making those two objects equivalent. Nor is Dymaxion distortion-free: projection cuts and distortion still matter to density, area, and distance comparisons. [BFI: Dymaxion Map](https://www.bfi.org/about-fuller/big-ideas/dymaxion-map/).

The tetrahedra and octahedra connect to the structural vocabulary of the octet truss. MoMA describes the truss as a tetrahedron–octahedron compound and dates its patent to 1961. The spherical triangular frameworks connect to geodesic constructions. These are concrete geometric correspondences; no claim is made that a photographed statistical chart is itself an octet truss. [MoMA: Airplane Hangar project](https://www.moma.org/collection/works/82398).

The packing, nested solids, and transformations are consistent with the investigations later assembled as *Synergetics* in 1975 and *Synergetics 2* in 1979, developed with E. J. Applewhite. Fuller’s recorded explanations also discuss vector equilibrium, packing, and the jitterbug transformation. These sources support contextual comparison, but an unlabeled animation study should not be positively identified as a jitterbug solely from a rotational resemblance. [BFI: Synergetics](https://www.bfi.org/about-fuller/big-ideas/synergetics/), [Fuller, *Everything I Know*, section 6](https://www.bfi.org/about-fuller/everything-i-know/section-6/).

The maps, inventories, forecasts, and seminar photographs connect to the Geoscope and World Game as environments for exploring planetary conditions and alternatives. World Game’s wider purpose included relating resources and capabilities to unmet needs. The Earth Metabolic Design leaflet, `115749657`, is unusually direct local evidence: it describes a comprehensive information system and models for testing strategies, organized across several areas of human need and the environment. [BFI: Geoscope](https://www.bfi.org/about-fuller/big-ideas/geoscope/), [BFI: World Game](https://www.bfi.org/about-fuller/big-ideas/world-game/).

### Five inferences from the expanded material

1. **This is partly an archive of making explanations.** Registration marks, paste-up labels, positive/negative variants, film credits, and title cards show production work alongside finished visualizations. High confidence. A sheet’s empty compartment may be awaiting another layer or production stage; blank does not establish a measured zero.

2. **The chart, map, and glyphs were complementary interfaces.** The chart favors systematic comparison; the map favors location and connection; the enlarged glyph favors comprehension of a record. Moderate-to-high confidence from the repeated subjects and keys. Exact synchronization among versions is not established.

3. **Normalization was central to the argument about provision.** Totals and per-capita measures explicitly coexist, and the circular studies name a USA reference. High confidence in the presence of normalization; moderate confidence about its full application. A contemporary design should make the selected benchmark contestable and visible rather than present it as a universal standard of wellbeing.

4. **Motion was part of the graphical method.** The opening/closing notes and film materials support sequential explanation of geometry and global systems. High confidence. The practical software analogue is a stable object whose layers and state can change while preserving identity, with a static comparison available alongside animation.

5. **The system’s political assumptions need their own representation.** The whole-world frame encourages thinking across national boundaries, but resource abundance alone does not establish access, consent, distribution, or feasibility. This is a critical interpretation, supported by the resource/need framing, not a claim about an unread authorial statement. Modern versions should expose ownership, access constraints, ecological limits, and uncertainty alongside quantities.

### Five additions that would cover the archive’s major forms

**1. A reusable compound-glyph specification.** Define named slots, metric bindings, units, scale domains, denominators, labels, and missing-value treatments independently of placement. Support triangular records, paired triangles, and circular comparison compartments using the same record model. Generate the enlarged legend from those slot definitions. Preserve the current `count_share_matrix` as a convenient specialization: its assumption that each membership is no larger than one common denominator is useful for counts, but inappropriate for unrelated resource quantities.

For the archival key, reproduce the eleven labeled positions with unresolved numerical values left unresolved. For new analytical work, provide a compact two- or three-field view and an expanded record. A face or compartment is a fixed container unless the legend explicitly assigns area a quantitative meaning. If area encodes a nonnegative value, scale similar shapes by the square root of value; do not use linear radius scaling while describing area as proportional.

**2. A topology-aware polyhedral drawing module.** Represent vertices, edges, faces, orientation, and projection explicitly. Begin with tetrahedron, octahedron, icosahedron, cube, and cuboctahedron/vector equilibrium; add dodecahedron and nesting relationships as needed. Support visible/hidden edges, face labels, exploded views, and planar nets. Complete the existing octahedron entry point and reuse the icosahedron’s geometry where appropriate, while making camera and face identity reusable.

This would reproduce the wireframe and sphere/net relationships and provide inspectable polyhedral data glyphs. Hidden faces and foreshortening make freely rotating solids weak for precise comparisons, so always offer a labeled net or fixed orthographic panels. Polyhedral shape alone does not determine a network topology or a force-bearing structure.

**3. Integer-addressed triangular/hexagonal lattices and recursive layouts.** Use stable integer coordinates for planar packing; convert them to drawing coordinates only at emission. Add triangular subdivisions, rhombic patches, nested-square arrangements, and explicit parent/child metadata. Cartofreako already documents the need to canonicalize Izzi’s floating-point honeycomb output to avoid duplicate display centers. Generalizing that fix in the shared primitive would help both projects. Current consumer’s clustering notes.

These operations should distinguish a **display cell**, an **aggregation group**, and a **geographic H3 cell**. Their shapes may resemble one another, but their identities and area semantics differ. A decorative planar hexagon cannot substitute for H3 geometry, including its pentagons and spherical area variation.

**4. Independent radial gauges and reference profiles.** Adapt the circular consumption studies into equal-angle compartments with a separate 0–100% or reference-ratio scale for each field. Give every compartment a label and a benchmark mark. Values exceeding a benchmark need visible overflow, not silent clipping. Use bars or dots in the detail view for precise comparisons. The key distinction is independent indicators versus a mutually exclusive composition; the renderer should require the author to select which semantics apply.

**5. Registered layers and state sequences.** Provide stable IDs and transforms for base geometry, outlines, data layers, annotations, and selected states. Add explicit hold/transition timing for opening, closing, unfolding, and layer comparison. This follows the archive’s production sheets more closely than simply adding a spiral effect. Distinguish animation time from the date represented by the data. An interpolated graphic is not an intermediate observation; scenario changes must remain labeled.

### Implementation sequence and acceptance criteria

The first useful increment is **compound-glyph extraction plus paired roles**, built on the existing matrix and GeoJSON adapter. The second is **stable spatial aggregation and map/grid linkage**. Polyhedral meshes and registered animation then broaden the geometric repertoire without delaying useful comparisons.

For future implementation, acceptance should cover metric/legend agreement, missing versus zero, role-specific denominators, lossless geographic IDs, stable scope selection, deterministic lattice placement, and readable static output. Polyhedral meshes need topology checks, including face/edge consistency and Euler’s relation for the closed convex examples. Geographic overlays need seam handling and preservation of true coordinates. Existing matrix, graph, and multi-translation-unit checks should remain applicable. These are proposed checks; this report does not implement or claim to test new renderers.

## 3. Implications for cartofreako

### What is shared, and what has changed

Cartofreako is already much more than a modern Dymaxion outline. Its README documents six production projection families, forward/reverse operations, browser support, and layered products. Its resource catalog lists **59 definitions: 14 released resource metrics and 45 exploration-only entries**. The network-swarm implementation already combines all ten downloader fields with H3 grouping and projection-aware placement. These are inspected local capabilities, not assumptions based on the project name.

| Question | World Game material in this corpus | Current cartofreako | Consequence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |

| Where are resources and people? | Metals, food, population, power sources and transmission. | Released energy, food, fauna, flora, mineral, and human products. | Extend the existing catalog and definitions rather than propose resource mapping from scratch. |
| How do quantities relate? | Aggregate/per-capita records and reference-based profiles. | Mostly separate metric products; a multivariable network glyph exists. | Add coordinated record inspection and common geographic selection across compatible measures. |
| How do things move? | Food-shipping and electric-network sheets explicitly include connections. | Fiber-route and infrastructure passes exist; swarm input has no measured edges. | Distinguish physical route, trade relationship, capacity, and measured traffic in every view. |
| What happens under a different choice? | Forecast labels, network annotations, and documents proposing strategy testing. | Reviewed generation paths emphasize sourced snapshots; no comparable resource-allocation game was established in this review. | Add a separate scenario model with assumptions and constraints, then render its results. |
| How is the result communicated? | Large shared displays, seminars, print, and film. | Reproducible SVG/PDF/raster products and browser interfaces. | Combine reproducible snapshots with shared selection, explanation, and scenario comparison. |

The principal missing layer is **a model connecting supply, need, access, transfer, and consequences**. More map layers alone cannot supply that model. For example, installed solar capacity is not generated electricity; a food-production index is not calories available to residents; a mineral deposit is not refined supply; a submarine cable’s route is not measured traffic. The existing resource implementation already makes several such distinctions and should remain the basis of richer views.

A practical extension would begin with one well-defined resource and compatible periods/geographies. Show supply, demand, a documented conversion or loss model, relevant connections, and a shortage/surplus calculation. Let a scenario alter a named assumption and compare the resulting map and table. Label proposed connections separately from existing infrastructure, and present unmet needs by location rather than only a world total. A full global optimizer is not required for the first useful version.

### Why a contemporary system differs

The archive already understands global interdependence, ecological consequences, and information as essential to planning. It would be inaccurate to claim those concerns are new. What has changed is the operational environment: geographically indexed digital observations can be versioned, recalculated, joined, and delivered through ordinary browsers. Cartofreako’s pinned-source generation and projection APIs make that difference concrete.

At the same time, digital communication has become a material resource system in its own right. Fiber, compute, electricity, spectrum, satellites, and access services create dependencies that should be mapped together. The IEA evidence above makes the energy/compute connection explicit, while ITU’s connectivity reporting distinguishes presence from affordable, useful access.

More observations also create more incompatible clocks, definitions, and coverage boundaries. The local catalog includes a 2011 reef-threat assessment alongside substantially newer energy and population measures. Rendering them together must preserve those dates. A current-looking map is not evidence that every layer describes the current world.

### What is likely to matter in 2036 and 2051

These are **planning judgments for ten and twenty-five years after 2026**, informed by the sources above. They are not precise forecasts of technology, politics, or environmental outcomes.

| Horizon | Likely analytical emphasis | Useful capability to build now |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 2036 | Provision under interacting constraints: grid and storage limits, cooling water, food/water stress, material processing, and affordable connectivity. | Join compatible resource/access indicators, express uncertainty, and compare local shortages against aggregate abundance. |
| 2036 | Reliability of interconnected physical and digital infrastructure. | Record verified dependencies and time-varying service availability. Analyze alternate routes only where topology and capacity are known. |
| 2051 | Adaptation and infrastructure lifetimes under differing climate and population pathways. | Store scenario ensembles, geographic changes, uncertainty ranges, and cumulative commitments; compare outcomes without erasing assumptions. |
| 2051 | Maintenance, reuse, recycling, replacement, and ecological repair alongside new extraction and construction. | Distinguish stocks, flows, useful service, losses, and end-of-life pathways. Preserve lifecycle and ownership information. |
| Both | Who receives service and who bears cost, risk, or exclusion. | Retain distributional comparisons, transparent benchmarks, and local context rather than collapse everything into one “world progress” score. |

The architecture should tolerate disagreement about the future. Separate an observation from an assumption, a model run from its rendering, and a shared world picture from an agreed policy objective.

## 4. Five integrations for Alpha60

### First establish what the fields actually mean

The producer’s `swarm_datum` and serialization code places the ten fields inside **both** `Feature.properties.downloaders` and `Feature.properties.uploaders`. `size` is a count in that role’s aggregation, not bytes. In `ip_to_swarm_datum()`, one classified IP contributes `size = 1`; final counting and uniqueness depend on the aggregation scope. These counts do not establish distinct human beings. **Alpha60 also reports separate downloader and uploader transfer volumes in the `data_transfer` object of cumulative JSON files.** The ten-field geographic record and that collection-level transfer summary are complementary inputs.

For example, `zootopia-2-cumulative.json` reports the following for `sample_duration="2026-01-29-to-2026-07-29"` (182 sample days), with `data_version="2026-06-18"`:

| Role | Transfer-size field | Reported value | Reported unit |
| --- | --- | ---: | --- |
| Downloaders | `data_transfer.downloader_transfer_size` | 637054.8238828327 | `terabyte` |
| Uploaders | `data_transfer.uploader_transfer_size` | 35236.95893598945 | `terabyte` |

The current `serialize_transfer_size()` implementation obtains these quantities through `make_glyphs()` and `sum_glyphs()`: each torrent’s payload size is multiplied by its peer or seed count, then summed over the supplied coalesced collection. The cumulative serialization path supplies `wcllu`, its unique-BTIHA collection. The calculation is `sum(torrent_payload_bytes × role_count)`; unit constants use decimal gigabytes and terabytes. This is a derived transfer-volume measure based on sampled roles and payload sizes, rather than a byte-counter measurement of completed transfers. The available evidence supports using the published volume with that definition; this review did not regenerate the historical file from its source samples.

The same object includes a `CloudFront-1-year` cost comparison. In the inspected code, the formula is `transfer_GB × 0.005 + 168000 USD`, separately for each role, and it reproduces the file’s reported costs to floating-point precision. Treat these as modeled costs under the recorded formula, not actual expenditure or a verified current tariff. Neither the transfer total nor its cost is automatically a per-H3 or per-network-class measurement: geographic or classification breakdowns require compatible source-level aggregation.

| `tor_exit_nodes` | Separate `is_exit_node(ip)` check. | Slot 4: exit-list count/share, distinct from `tor`. |
| `vpn` | Privacy-database VPN field. | Slot 5: VPN classification. |
| `relay` | Privacy-database relay field. | Slot 6: relay classification. |
| `proxy` | Privacy-database proxy field. | Slot 7: proxy classification. |
| `hosting` | Privacy-database hosting field. | Slot 8: hosting classification. |
| `service` | The parser counts a nonempty service string; more generally its helper accepts Boolean true or nonempty strings. | Slot 9: named-service match, retaining the raw field name in details. Not a sum of other fields. |

The privacy behavior is visible in `privacy_segments::accumulate()` and `update_from_json()`. These are implementation semantics, not validation of upstream classification accuracy. Notably, the producer initializes fields to zero and conditionally runs certain lookups. **A serialized zero can therefore mean no match or no applicable lookup in that build/path.** A trustworthy new view needs backend availability/version and coverage metadata; the renderer cannot recover that distinction from the count alone.

Fields may overlap. Display them as independent indicators: do not use a pie, a 100% stack, or a ternary position to imply a partition. For role `r`, geographic group `g`, period `t`, and field `k`:

```text
N[r,g,t]    = reported size for that role, geography, and period
C[r,g,t,k]  = reported count for field k
P[r,g,t,k]  = 100 * C[r,g,t,k] / N[r,g,t], when inputs are valid and N > 0
```

The uploader denominator is uploader size; the downloader denominator is downloader size. For compatible, nonoverlapping aggregate records, compute a group share from the sum of counts divided by the sum of denominators, not the average of percentages. Even then the result inherits the producer’s uniqueness semantics. Do not infer intersections such as VPN-and-hosting from marginal counts.

### 1. Paired uploader/downloader records

Adapt the archive’s paired and mirrored triangular vocabulary to a fixed-size rhombus: upper triangle for uploaders, lower triangle for downloaders. Show both totals, then the same selected network indicator with separate role-specific percentages. Retain text labels and orientation cues so color is not the sole distinction. The archive’s mineral-symbol sketch, `133253869`, offers a visual reference for opposed forms, not a historical uploader/downloader encoding.

A larger detail view can repeat the nine network slots on each side. An optional difference `P[uploaders,k] − P[downloaders,k]` is a percentage-point contrast between role profiles. It is not net traffic. Likewise, `U/(U+D)` is only a role-count balance if its meaning is explicitly stated; roles need not represent disjoint people across a cumulative window.

At collection scope, add a separate paired **transfer-volume** view using `data_transfer.uploader_transfer_size` and `data_transfer.downloader_transfer_size`, normalized to the same unit. Give counts and volume distinct scales and labels, with the derivation and sample interval in the detail record. This extends the archive’s pairing of population and resource quantities: a role’s count and its payload-weighted volume answer different questions. Link the collection summary to geographic views without distributing its volume among locations in proportion to their counts; torrent sizes and memberships can differ by location.

Two current constraints matter. The Izzi adapter selects and sorts top rows separately for the chosen category, so two independently rendered matrices need not contain the same places. Use one explicit shared set and order. More fundamentally, `serialize_swarm_features()` in the inspected Alpha60 producer emits a location only when its **downloader** count reaches `geomin`; it serializes uploaders inside those retained features. An uploader comparison made from that output is conditioned on downloader visibility. For an uploader-complete view, obtain an export with an appropriate union/role selection policy, or label the retained support explicitly. A frontend cannot recover excluded places.

### 3. One record in two coordinated places: Dymaxion and grid

Keep the compact map glyph to total plus one or two selected indicators. All ten fields belong in the enlarged view or matrix. ### 5. Time-indexed swarm atlas with stable identity

Arrange rows by a fixed geographic unit or explicitly selected swarm group, columns by observation window, and role as paired records or facets. Within each record, keep the same network slots. ### Delivery and verification discipline

Keep each repository increment small enough to review and retain the fixtures and expected outputs that establish its semantics. Run affected existing checks and the focused new acceptance checks at each stage; broaden testing when shared interfaces change. Record cross-repository schema/API versions in the integration example so an older exporter does not silently acquire newer semantics. A stage is complete when its stated artifact and checks are available, not when its interface names have been added.
