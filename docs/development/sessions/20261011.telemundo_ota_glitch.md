### Reference shader formula: colors, mixed blocks, horizontal tears

`decoder_reference_movie.py`
is the final photographic shader. Its 32 weighted BGR centroids
come from eight frames at `8 + j/8` seconds, `j=0…7`, of
`IMG_10-12-2022_10-10-49_8.1.101.mp4`. Sampling uses every sixth row and column;
OpenCV k-means uses seed 20261011, three attempts, 80 iterations, epsilon 0.1.
The reference receipt
pins the recording's hash and extraction. Scene pixels come from the pool.

With `detail_orientation=1`, rotate photographs counterclockwise for the
shader's landscape working domain, then rotate the result back to portrait.
Build geometry on a 960 × 540 reference field and enlarge it to the 3840 × 2160
working photograph. The field combines 13 × 9, 43 × 31, and 170 × 108 noise
scales, warped 11 × 8 regional palette samples, and **6,800 overlapping patches**.
Five patch classes use width ranges `[1,7)`, `[4,19)`, `[12,49)`, `[30,130)`,
`[80,350)` and height ranges `[1,3)`, `[2,7)`, `[3,17)`, `[8,45)`, `[1,4)`
on that reference field, with probabilities `0.39, 0.30, 0.18, 0.10, 0.03`.
Patch widths are multiplied by `0.8 + detail_block_pixels/24` and rounded.

The palette selector combines the three noise scales with weights
`0.57 / 0.24 / 0.19`, ranks their values, and samples the centroid-weight CDF.
Base color is `0.82*warped_regional_color + 0.18*CDF_color`. A patch selects a
fresh weighted centroid with probability 0.16 and otherwise uses its regional
color; its color blend is 0.7 for the first two size classes and 0.3 for the
other classes. The initial prediction-offset field spans ±0.095 of width and
±0.045 of height; patch offsets span ±0.16 of width and ±0.07 of height.
Historical sampling is bilinear with wrapped coordinates. The fine residue
field sums centered noise at 480 × 540, 210 × 540, and 320 × 180 scales,
with amplitudes `42 / 45 / 35`, respectively.

The PCG64 field stream uses `(native_seed XOR checkpoint*0x9e3779b97f4a7c15)
mod 2^64`; the tear stream uses `(native_seed + checkpoint*101) mod 2^64`.
In the following formula, `P` is the displaced historical photograph, `F`
and `B` are its grayscale residuals after Gaussian blur with sigma
`max(1,2*w/1920)` and `max(1,17*w/1920)`, and `E` is the multiscale residue:

```text
a = detail_amount * native_severity
residue = detail_coefficients * (1.1*F + 0.25*B + E)
corrupted = clip(palette_field + residue, 0, 255)
damaged = uint8(clip((1-detail_chroma)*P + detail_chroma*corrupted, 0, 255))
output[damage_mask < a] = damaged[damage_mask < a]
wide_horizontal_tears = round(detail_slices * a * 7)
short_slice_fragments = round(detail_slices * a * 900)
```

Wide tears shift completed picture bands by up to ±0.24 of the working width,
with varied band heights, narrow dark seams, and occasional repeated rows.
Short fragments have lognormal lengths and varied one-to-five-pixel nominal
heights. Rolling reacquisition clears the upper portion of the damage mask.
The native blank and hold policies are applied before this shader; zero
severity retains the current photograph. This is the chosen artistic decoder
interpretation; the reference recording does not establish the transmitted
codec or RF cause.

Three options, in increasing scope:

1. **Reuse existing satellite constructions first.** Combine `mpeg-picture-damage` for sustained corruption with `transport-burst-recovery` for intermittent loss. This gives a useful approximation immediately, although the current renderer only replaces regions with historical imagery—it does not produce the samples’ displaced fragments or vivid color corruption. 2. **Enrich the shared receiver with slice damage and prediction drift — recommended.** Add horizontal runs of damaged blocks, displaced historical fragments, and damage that persists until a refresh. Proposed controls: `slice_run_blocks`, `displacement_x`, `displacement_y`, `damage_persistence_frames`, and `refresh_mode`. Treat localized chroma corruption as an optional artistic extension. This most directly addresses the samples’ distinctive appearance. MPEG-2 documentation describes previous-picture substitution, motion-based concealment, slice resynchronization, and temporal recovery, providing a sound basis for these interpretations. [ITU-T H.262, Annex D.13](https://www.itu.int/rec/T-REC-H.262-200002-S/en)

3. **Add loss-of-lock and reacquisition sequences.** Allow explicit transitions between damaged regions, full-picture hold, blanking, and recovery, with configurable burst spacing and duration. The current transport profile hardcodes 0.55-second windows every two seconds, which cannot closely follow these recordings. Optional receiver-message overlays could reproduce the observed display behavior. Digital reception can fail abruptly when error correction is overwhelmed; multipath and burst noise are documented reception impairments. [ATSC A/111](https://www.atsc.org/wp-content/uploads/2015/03/Design-of-multiple-transmitter-networks.pdf), [ATSC A/74](https://www.atsc.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/04/A74-2010.pdf)

I recommend **option 1 as the baseline, then option 2 for visual fidelity**, with option 3 optional. Keep the engine shared and identify OTA through the TV-broadcast family and a descriptive profile such as `ota-decoder-breakup-v1`.
